## nongrav

**Headline.** Non-gravitational fits are a narrow but real channel: JPL already publishes them for only 174 of 7,226 NHATS objects (16 of the 286 with delta-v under 5 km/s), and the density upper bound from a Yarkovsky drift is valid but constrains rho times D, so today it robustly excludes metal for about 11 NHATS objects. The open gap is calibrated inference on a few hundred objects, with injection-recovery and stated false-positive rates; it is not the orbit fitting, which JPL and ESA NEOCC both already do.

LIVE COUNTS (JPL SBDB and NHATS APIs, 2026-11-20). 989 small bodies have any of A1/A2/A3 (625 asteroids, 364 comets). Among NEOs: 686 (623 asteroids, 63 comets); A1 69 (21 asteroids, 48 comets), A2 680 (618 asteroids, 62 comets), A3 24 (9 asteroids, 15 comets). Of the 618 NEO asteroids with A2, 573 are at 3 sigma or better, 351 at 5 sigma, 146 at 10 sigma; 445 negative versus 173 positive. The query misses custom models (Bennu carries AMRAT/RHO, not A2). NHATS returned 7,226 objects; 174 have a fitted parameter (A1 18, A2 169, A3 7): 8 of 40 with delta-v under 4 km/s, 16 of 286 under 5, 28 of 726 under 6. ESA NEOCC's catalogue flags 391 of 43,146 NEAs with a non-grav model (104 in NHATS) and publishes per-object files with area-to-mass ratio, A2 and covariance; it solves an area-to-mass ratio for 8 objects, all NHATS.

CORRECTION TO THE BRIEF. arXiv 2502.13222 is Fenucci et al. 2025 on Kamo'oalewa. The NEOCC pipeline is arXiv 2311.10175 (A&A 682, A29): 32,396 NEAs, 5,308 candidates, 461 at S/N 3 or more, 348 accepted.

YARKOVSKY PHYSICS. Linear theory: da/dt = -(8/9)(1-A)(Phi/n) W(Theta) cos(gamma), Phi = 3F/(2 D rho c), |W| = 0.5 Theta/(1 + Theta + 0.5 Theta^2) for large bodies, maximum 0.2071 at Theta = sqrt(2). With |cos gamma| at most 1 this gives rho_max [kg/m3] = 3.128e-8 (1-A) / (D[m] |A2|[au/d2]). The idea is published (Vokrouhlicky et al. 2015: the peak "often allows strict upper bounds on rho", citing Chesley et al. 2003) and is used in reverse to reject detections (Desmars 2015, Del Vigna 2018, Fenucci 2024). I found nobody applying it across a population as a composition screen. Allowances: finite-size peak 0.2455 (my computation from a recalled formula), roughness "tens of percent", nonlinearity up to 15%; a conservative bound is about 1.5 times the nominal one at |A2| minus 2 sigma. Validation on 12 objects with independent densities: Bennu 0.87 of the bound, Ryugu 0.87, Itokawa 0.65, eight binaries 0.30 to 0.88; one violation, the M-type contact binary (153201) 2000 WO107 (4.8 g/cm3 versus a nominal bound of 3,640 kg/m3), which NEOCC rejects as too large for Yarkovsky. 1986 DA, 2016 ED85 and Amun have no fitted drift anywhere. Only about 50 of the detections have a spin pole and 192 a measured diameter (SsODNet); 37 have pole, diameter and a 3-sigma A2. Worked examples: H = 22.5 with |A2| = 1e-13 gives rho_max 1,632 to 4,237 kg/m3 for albedo 0.05 to 0.5; H = 27.5 with 1e-12 gives the same numbers. On today's data 118 of 154 NHATS detections have nominal rho_max under 4,000 at albedo 0.15, but only 11 under the conservative bound at albedo 0.30. Dominant errors: unknown size, thermal inertia for fast rotators (a factor up to 40), obliquity.

RADIATION PRESSURE. A1 [au/d2] = 2.266e-7 C_R AMR [m2/kg]; rho = 1.5/(AMR D). Published: 2009 BD (2.97 +/- 0.33)e-4 m2/kg; 2011 MD (3.2 +/- 0.6)e-4 and 1.1 (+0.7/-0.5) g/cm3 with Spitzer; 2012 LA; 2015 TC25; 2012 TC4; 2020 CD3 (6.9 +/- 2.4)e-4; 2021 GM1; 2024 PT5 disputed (Bolin 7.0e-5 versus Kareta 0.0 +/- 1.3e-4). Unpublished in SBDB: 2020 GE (implied density under 0.55 g/cm3), 2026 QQ8, 2021 VH2. All have H of 26.7 or fainter and slow Earth encounters; arcs 73 days to 5 years. Pool: 2,370 NHATS objects have H of 27 or more, 59 of them arcs of 100 days or more, 49 without A1. My estimate is 5 to 15 new detections plus 30 to 100 useful upper limits. Metal bodies will not be detected; they yield upper limits. A size-free test: for radiative forces |A2|/A1 must be under about 0.09; 2006 RH120 is at 0.33.

DARK COMETS. Fourteen published (Seligman 2023, 2024), plus 1998 SH2, confirmed as comet P/1998 SH2 (Farnocchia et al. 2026, Nature Astronomy). Eight of the nine inner dark comets are NHATS objects. No gas or dust has been seen on any inner one. 1998 KY26 is Xe-type, 11 m, albedo 0.52, too warm for buried ice (Hansen and Hirabayashi 2026), and its out-of-plane term has fallen from 2.70e-11 +/- 0.65e-11 (4.2 sigma, published) to 1.69e-12 +/- 1.12e-12 (1.5 sigma) in the current JPL solution. No systematic all-NEO or NHATS search with pre-registered thresholds and false-positive control exists; the JPL group proposed one in July 2026. A crude screen on public A2 values recovers every known case with an A2 and flags 2016 TA56, 2006 VC, 2011 CL50 and 2003 LN6.

ANOMALY. 2022 OB5 (delta-v 3.52 km/s, former AstroForge target, X-complex, 1.54-minute rotator): JPL A2 = -1.66e-12 at 26 sigma; NEOCC fits a negative area-to-mass ratio, -2.37e-4 +/- 0.55e-4 m2/kg.

SURVEYS. Gaia DR4 is not out: planned 2 December 2026, 12:00 CET; 66 months; 436,551 solar-system objects, 60.5 million epoch observations. The 66-month astrometry for 156,764 asteroids was already in the 2023 Focused Product Release (43 robust Yarkovsky detections among 446 NEAs). No published forecast of DR4 or Rubin Yarkovsky counts was found. Rubin simulations give about 885 NEOs under 10 m with arcs of two years or more.

### Prior art

- **Fenucci et al. 2024, A&A 682, A29: automated Yarkovsky detection at ESA NEOCC**: Ran a transverse-acceleration fit on 5,308 of 32,396 NEAs, accepted 348 detections using S/N of 3 or more, a test against the 95th percentile of the modelled maximum drift, and an isolated-tracklet test. Results are republished monthly with per-object orbit files. Overlap: Very high for the fitting step and for A2. It already contains the maximum-drift logic, used to throw anomalies away. It does not search A1 or A3 systematically, does no injection-recovery, and derives no densities. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10175)
- **JPL Small-Body Database orbit solutions**: Publishes A1/A2/A3 with uncertainties for 623 NEO asteroids, including the dark comets, with undocumented criteria for when parameters are added. Overlap: This is the project's main input and its benchmark. Any refit must reproduce it before claiming anything new. (https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/sbdb_query.html)
- **Vokrouhlicky et al. 2015, 'The Yarkovsky and YORP Effects' (Asteroids IV); Chesley et al. 2003, 2014**: Gives the linear theory, states that the peak of the density versus thermal-inertia relation allows strict upper bounds on density, and shows the Bennu case (1,260 +/- 70 predicted, 1,190 measured). Overlap: The density upper bound is their idea, stated for single objects with known spin and size. Not applied as a population screen. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.01249)
- **Greenberg et al. 2020, AJ 159, 92**: Drift rates for 247 NEAs and a 'Yarkovsky efficiency' for each, using assumed densities and sizes. Overlap: The efficiency is the inverse of the project's bound. They did not use the theoretical maximum of about 0.09 or infer densities. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05513)
- **Novakovic et al. 2024 (ASTERIA) and Fenucci et al. 2021, 2023, 2025**: Monte Carlo inversion of a measured drift for thermal inertia, with population priors on size, density, obliquity and period; validated on Bennu plus 10 NEAs, applied to 38. Overlap: Same statistical machinery the project would need, aimed at thermal inertia with density as a prior. Swapping the target to density or composition class is a small step they have not published. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03479)
- **Dziadura et al. 2023 and 2025 (Gaia DR3 and FPR)**: Fitted 446 NEAs with Gaia astrometry, 49 then 43 robust detections, and computed bulk densities for the 2023 sample. Overlap: Already produced Yarkovsky-based densities for NEAs and already exploited the 66-month Gaia astrometry that DR4 will re-release. Not focused on accessible or small objects. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09750)
- **Mommert et al. 2014a,b and Micheli et al. 2012, 2014**: Measured radiation pressure on 2009 BD and 2011 MD from astrometry, then combined Spitzer photometry with an orbital model containing radiation pressure and Yarkovsky to get joint probabilistic size, albedo, density and thermal inertia. Overlap: This is the per-object product the project wants, done for two Asteroid Redirect Mission candidates. It is the closest thing to a resource-motivated use of the channel. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.7699)
- **Seligman et al. 2023 (PSJ) and 2024 (PNAS); Farnocchia et al. 2023**: Defined dark comets and listed fourteen with fitted accelerations and dust limits. Overlap: Covers the anomalous-acceleration idea, including eight NHATS objects. Selection was case by case with no stated thresholds or false-alarm control, which is the opening. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07603)
- **Farnocchia et al. 2026, Nature Astronomy (1998 SH2)**: Predicted cometary activity from an acceleration ten times the Yarkovsky maximum, confirmed it by deep imaging, and proposed screening all NEOs' motion. Overlap: The JPL group has publicly proposed the all-NEO search. They are the likeliest to publish it first. (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-026-02913-7)
- **Taylor et al. 2024, ApJ (misidentification) and Hung et al. 2023, PSJ (main belt)**: Synthetic-data tests: linking failure under strong accelerations; detectability of Yarkovsky for 134 main-belt asteroids. Overlap: The only injection-style tests found. Neither measures detection efficiency or false-alarm rate for A1/A2/A3 on NEA astrometry. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06874)
- **Desmars 2015 and Del Vigna et al. 2018**: Yarkovsky lists (46 and 87) filtered by comparing the measured drift with a size-based maximum or expected value; Del Vigna also reports four radiation-pressure detections. Overlap: Earlier uses of the maximum-drift comparison as a spuriousness filter. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05947)
- **Krugly et al. 2025, A&A (2000 WO107)**: Shape model and density of 4.8 g/cm3 for an M-type contact-binary NEA, after an earlier Yarkovsky-based estimate from the same group suggested a low density. Overlap: The only metal-type NEA where a Yarkovsky drift and an independent density can be compared. They disagree at nominal values. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.04535)
- **Alarcon et al. 2026, Icarus (2022 OB5)**: Found a 1.54-minute rotation and X-complex colours for the former commercial mining target and argued accessibility is not exploitability; left density at 1,800 to 4,900 kg/m3. Overlap: Shows the resource literature does not use the non-gravitational data already public for the same object. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04784)

### Opportunities

- **Publish, for every NHATS object with a 3-sigma A2 (154 today), a posterior on rho times D and on density given explicit priors on albedo, obliquity, thermal inertia and rotation period, with a nominal and a conservative upper bound and a 'metal excluded' flag.** New: The bound is published as a principle and used to reject detections, but no one has applied it object by object as a composition screen, and not to the accessible population. Feasibility: High. It can start from public SBDB and NEOCC values with no orbit fitting; I ran a first pass today in minutes. It improves sharply wherever another channel supplies a size. Risk: Without sizes it robustly excludes metal for only about 11 objects, and the single metal known-answer case (2000 WO107) does not validate cleanly.
- **Use the same statistic as an anomaly flag: objects whose drift would need a density below any plausible value are dark-comet candidates or bad fits.** New: No published systematic screen exists. The crude version recovers all five outer dark comets, 1998 SH2 and 2006 RH120 from public numbers and flags 2016 TA56, 2006 VC, 2011 CL50 and 2003 LN6. Feasibility: High for the screen. Confirming any candidate needs a careful refit and ideally deep imaging by others. Risk: The JPL group proposed this in July 2026 and may publish first; flagged objects may already be known to them or be artefacts.
- **Independent open refit of A1, A2 and A3 for the roughly 150 to 350 NHATS objects with adequate arcs, gated on reproducing the JPL and NEOCC values for the 270 or so objects both publish.** New: Two professional solutions exist but disagree by policy on anomalous objects, and neither documents thresholds for A1 and A3. An open, versioned third solution with stated criteria does not exist. Feasibility: Medium to low. It needs an orbit-determination stack with star-catalogue debiasing, station weighting, radar and close-approach dynamics. The known-object gate is clear and hard. Risk: A fitter that is nearly right manufactures false 3 to 5 sigma detections. This is the step most likely to fail quietly.
- **Injection-recovery on real observation sets: add synthetic A1/A2/A3 to each object's actual epochs and error model, refit, and publish detection efficiency and false-alarm rate against arc length, H and observation count, with a trials correction for fitting three terms.** New: Nothing of this kind was found for NEA non-gravitational detections. The 1998 KY26 case shows why it is needed. Feasibility: Medium. It depends on the refit pipeline but is then mostly compute. Risk: Real errors are non-Gaussian and station-dependent, so a simple noise model will understate false alarms.
- **Area-to-mass ratios and upper limits for small NHATS objects with long arcs (49 with H of 27 or more and arcs of 100 days or more that have no A1 yet), reporting non-detections as density lower limits.** New: Published cases number about ten and were done one at a time. Upper limits, which are what metal-rich objects would give, are not published at all. Feasibility: Medium. Pool is small and well defined; JPL has already taken the easy detections. Risk: Density still needs a size. Expect perhaps 5 to 15 new detections, so the science is in the limits.
- **A size-independent consistency test for objects with both terms: for purely radiative forces |A2|/A1 must be below about 0.09.** New: Not found stated as a test. It separates 2006 RH120 (0.33) from the ordinary cases (0.01 to 0.03) with no size or density assumption. Feasibility: High, but only about ten objects have both terms. Risk: A1 and A2 are correlated on short arcs, so the ratio inherits fit degeneracies.
- **Lead case studies for the website: 2022 OB5 (negative area-to-mass ratio, 26-sigma drift, commercial target), 1998 KY26 (out-of-plane term gone in the current solution), 2020 GE (implied density under 0.55 g/cm3), 2000 WO107 (metal test), and an upper limit on the drift of 1986 DA.** New: None of these specific findings appears in the literature I could search, and each is checkable against public data. Feasibility: Medium. Each is a single-object refit with independent cross-checks available. Risk: Any of them may dissolve under a proper refit, or be known already to the specialist groups. They must be framed as tests, with null results published.

### Reasons it fails

- Coverage: only 174 of 7,226 NHATS objects (2.4%) have any fitted non-gravitational parameter, and 97% of NHATS objects with H of 27 or more have arcs under 60 days, so the density column would be empty for almost the whole catalog.
- The Yarkovsky bound constrains rho times D. With size taken from H and an unknown albedo the bound moves by a factor 2.6, and with modelling allowances it loosens by about 1.5 more; robust metal exclusion holds for about 11 NHATS objects today.
- Metal-rich bodies are the class this channel sees worst: high density and high conductivity suppress both the drift and the radiation-pressure signal, so they appear only as non-detections. The detected sample is biased toward light, low-inertia, pole-aligned bodies, and the known metal-rich NEAs (1986 DA, 2016 ED85, Amun) have no fitted drift at all.
- The one metal-type NEA with both a drift and an independent density (2000 WO107) violates the nominal bound, and its density is itself model-dependent, so the method has no clean known-answer validation for the metal class.
- Marginal detections evaporate: 1998 KY26's defining out-of-plane term went from 4.2 sigma in print to 1.5 sigma in the current JPL solution. Fitting three terms on fewer than 100 observations and keeping the significant one needs a trials correction nobody has applied.
- Radiation pressure and transverse terms trade off on short sparse arcs: NEOCC's fit for 2022 OB5 returns a negative area-to-mass ratio at 4.3 sigma with correlation 0.80 to A2. A2-only fits on metre-scale objects may be absorbing other forces.
- Fast-rotator thermal physics is unsettled: published fits need thermal inertia of about 20 to 100 SI on minute-scale rotators. If that reflects missing physics, density bounds for exactly the small accessible objects are wrong.
- An anomalous acceleration is not a water detection. No gas or dust has been seen on any inner dark comet, the best-studied one is Xe-type and too warm for buried ice, and only an outer, 380 m object has been confirmed active. Presenting 'dark comet' as a volatile indicator would overstate the evidence.
- Orbit determination at JPL fidelity is specialist work (debiasing, per-station weights, timing errors, radar, perturbers, co-orbital dynamics). An agent-built fitter that is slightly wrong will create false detections that look like discoveries.
- The search itself is likely to be done by others: NEOCC already runs a monthly A2 pipeline over all NEAs and the JPL group proposed all-NEO screening in July 2026.
- Circular validation: most densities catalogued in SsODNet for these objects were derived from the Yarkovsky drift, so they cannot be used to validate a Yarkovsky density method. Truly independent densities number about a dozen, all much larger than NHATS targets.
- Gaia DR4 adds almost nothing directly for NHATS-size objects (they are too faint for Gaia), and Rubin's multi-year arcs on small NEOs cannot exist before about 2028.

### Open questions

- Which open orbit-determination code can reproduce JPL and NEOCC A2 values within 1 sigma on the common set of about 270 objects? I did not evaluate candidates.
- What is the true maximum Yarkovsky efficiency once finite size, roughness and nonlinearity are included? My finite-size value of 0.2455 comes from a recalled formula and needs checking against Vokrouhlicky (1998, 1999); one number with an uncertainty should be fixed and pre-registered.
- Does the collapse of 1998 KY26's out-of-plane term hold when all of the roughly 260 measurements now available are used, and are the dark-comet authors aware of it?
- What explains the negative area-to-mass ratio in NEOCC's solution for 2022 OB5, and does a joint fit with physical constraints (A1 non-negative, |A2|/A1 under 0.1) still fit the astrometry?
- Is 2020 GE natural? Its area-to-mass ratio implies a density below 0.55 g/cm3 for any albedo up to 0.5.
- Are 2016 TA56 and 2011 CL50 already tracked as dark-comet candidates by the JPL group?
- What upper limit on A2 do existing data give for 1986 DA and 2016 ED85, and is it below the roughly 5e-15 au/d2 expected for a stony body of the same size?
- How many of the 174 NHATS objects with non-gravitational fits have an independent size (NEOWISE, Spitzer, radar)? That decides how often rho times D becomes rho.
- Will the Gaia DR4 performance paper on small bodies (2 December 2026) publish its own Yarkovsky list, and how many NEAs are among the 436,551 objects?
- Is there any forecast, published or not, of Rubin's yield of non-gravitational detections for small NEOs?
- Is the 0.5% to 60% dark-comet fraction actually stated in Taylor et al. 2024, and on what basis?

## source-prior

**Headline.** For NHATS objects the orbit -> source -> family -> meteorite chain collapses to a constant: nu6 is the top source for more than 99.9% of them in both public models, the orbit carries 0.003-0.005 bits about albedo class out of about 1.4, and a direct test on known objects gives AUC 0.47-0.50. The plan should use an H-binned empirical base rate plus a lunar/artificial origin mixture for the Earth-like corner, and present the null as a result.

VERDICT. For the NHATS population, the chain orbit -> source region -> family -> meteorite type gives a base rate, not a per-object discriminator. Most of the machinery already exists in public form.

WHAT I RAN TODAY (the report lists the scripts and per-object CSVs). I computed source probabilities for 7,220 NHATS objects from the two public tables: NEOMOD (Model136, the table behind give_me_prob.f) and the Granvik et al. 2018 low-resolution cell file.
- nu6 is the top source for more than 99.9% of NHATS objects in both models. NEOMOD mean vector: nu6 0.81, 3:1 0.14, inner weak resonances 0.04, all else below 0.01; P(nu6) 10th-90th percentile 0.72-0.88. Granvik: nu6 0.70, Hungaria 0.215, 3:1 0.09. Alessi & Jedicke 2026 independently get 78/16/5% for 97 Earth co-orbitals.
- Mutual information between orbit cell and albedo class inside NHATS: 0.003-0.005 bits of about 1.4 (0.2-0.4%). P(pV <= 0.1) from Granvik+Morbidelli spans 0.090-0.115 (5th-95th percentile). For all NEOs it is 0.07-0.08 bits.
- Even a perfectly known escape route gives only 0.04-0.13 bits with Marsset et al. 2022 debiased fractions, because nu6 and 3:1J are nearly identical (S/Q 44.4% vs 43.3%; carbonaceous 45% vs 51%; Pearson 99.8%).
- Recover-known-objects test on SBDB: AUC for dark vs bright is 0.63 over 1,204 NEOs with albedos, 0.50 for a < 2 au, 0.47 for the 69 NHATS members; log-loss skill +5%, -0.6%, -0.5%. Partly circular (models tuned on NEOWISE), so these are upper bounds.
- METEOMOD family model on NHATS orbits: ordinary chondrite 68%, hydrated carbonaceous 25%, HED 3%, metal about 1%. Its apparent 0.11 bits falls to 0.03-0.06 after rebinning; about half is map noise.

MODELS DISAGREE ON THE BASE RATE MORE THAN OBJECTS DIFFER. P(dark or carbonaceous) for an NHATS-type object: about 5% (NEOMOD3 per-source dark fractions: nu6 0.037, 3:1 0.069), 10% (Granvik/Morbidelli), 25% (METEOMOD), 39% (Marsset mapping), 67% (Lopez-Oquendo et al. 2026, debiased C/X below 150 m). Hungaria share: 21.5% (Granvik) vs 0.7% (NEOMOD). Part of the spread is conditioning on H versus on diameter (about a factor of 6 in dark-to-bright odds). Per-object priors must be H-conditioned.

PRIOR ART. Public tools map orbit to escape route (Granvik files at Helsinki, citation-only; NEOMOD simulator at SwRI, no licence text; ESA NEOPOP 2.1; Lowell astorb), to albedo class (columns in the Granvik file; NEOMOD3 per-source dark fractions), and to family and meteorite class (METEOMOD web forms and an 18.7 MB tarball, 52 families). MANOS has characterised about 400 NHATS-type objects: 210 spectra (Devogele 2019: S+Q 43.8 +/- 4.6%, X 23.8%, C 10.9%; mean H 25, mean delta-v 5.7 km/s) and 189 colours (Moskovitz 2026: S+Q about 70% at H = 14 falling to about 35% at H = 28 in a pooled 831-object sample), and states source region is not the dominant factor. Binzel 2019: the delta-V <= 7 km/s subset is "a representative mix".

2024 METEORITE RESULTS. Broz 2024 (Nature): about 70% of falls from Karin (5.8 Myr), Koronis2 (7.6 Myr) and Massalia2 (< about 40 Myr). At 1 km: LL 63, H 13, L 11, HED 12%. At 1 m: H 40, L 47, LL 7, HED 6%. Marsset 2024: L chondrites from Massalia. Broz 2024 (A&A): CM from Veritas and Konig, CI from Polana and Euphrosyne, CO/CV/CK from Eos; pre-atmospheric carbonaceous flux about equal to ordinary chondrite flux at 1 m, against 4.4% vs 80% of falls. Contested at family level: Jenniskens & Devillepoix 2025 put L chondrites at Hertha/Nysa and some H at Massalia. Sanchez 2024 finds small NEOs LL-dominated (61%); Simon 2026 finds a shift toward H and L below 100 m.

LOW DELTA-V. 87% of NHATS objects with delta-v < 5 km/s (median a 1.02, e 0.083, i 2.2 deg; 74% with H >= 27) lie in Jedicke's lunar-ejecta box; 46% of all NHATS lie where NEOMOD2 under-predicts small NEOs by up to about 30% (attributed to tidal disruption). Alessi & Jedicke 2026: nominal 71 lunar vs about 1,600 main-belt co-orbitals above 10 m (about 4%), with +/- 5 orders of magnitude, lunar dominant as e and i go to zero. 2024 PT5 is accepted as lunar; Kamo'oalewa is disputed (Fenucci 2026: main belt 1.23 +/- 0.13 vs Giordano Bruno 0.042; Zhang 2026: LL chondrite, Flora). Tianwen-2 arrived June-July 2026 (first image 6 July; body about 16-27 m). I found no composition result or sampling report as of 2026-11-20; sample return is planned for November 2027. Artificial contaminants: 2020 SO, 2018 CN41.

LABELLED DATA. 61 falls with orbits (meteoriteorbits.info, 22 Sep 2026: 19 H, 19 L, 6 LL, 7 carbonaceous, 5 achondrites, 1 EL, 1 iron); 13 imminent impactors (4 typed: ureilite, HED, L, aubrite); 824 European Network fireballs with strength classes (CDS unreachable today); 1,074 CNEOS fireballs, 363 with velocities. Good as an external check; too small and too filtered (carbonaceous suppressed 15-40x) to train on.

RECOMMENDED PRIOR. Origin mixture (main belt / lunar / artificial) by Earth-likeness and size, then H-binned Dirichlet base rates from H-matched surveys (H 24-29: S+Q 35-45%, X 20-25% of unknown E/M/P split, C 11-15%, B+D about 5%, V about 5%, other 10-15%), with source-model vectors shown as sensitivity. Validate with a pre-registered hold-out skill test against the base rate (expect none at a < 2 au), NEOMOD3-based injection-recovery, a falls and impactors check, and dated forward predictions.

### Prior art

- **Granvik et al. 2018 NEO model with Morbidelli et al. 2020 albedo categories**: Seven escape routes, calibrated 17 < H < 25, published as a cell table with escape-route probabilities and albedo-category probabilities per (a, e, i, H) cell. Overlap: This is already a public 'albedo class given orbit' predictor. On NHATS it returns a near-constant 10% dark for every object. (https://www.mv.helsinki.fi/home/mgranvik/data/Granvik+_2018_Icarus/)
- **NEOMOD, NEOMOD2, NEOMOD3 (Nesvorny et al. 2023, 2024a, 2024b) and the NEOMOD Simulator**: Twelve-source debiased model to H = 28, with per-source albedo mixtures in NEOMOD3; Fortran simulator and a per-object source-probability lookup. Overlap: Supplies the source vector and a dark-fraction prior directly. Disagrees with Granvik on Hungaria by a factor of about 30 for NHATS objects and has no lunar or tidal source. (https://www.boulder.swri.edu/~davidn/NEOMOD_Simulator/)
- **METEOMOD and family-level NEOMOD (Broz et al. 2024)**: Probability of origin in each of 52 asteroid families from (a, i), with families labelled by meteorite class; web forms and downloadable maps. Overlap: This is the orbit -> family -> meteorite-type chain, already built, for 1 m and 1 km bodies. Maps are sparse and noisy at a near 1 au. (https://sirrah.troja.mff.cuni.cz/~mira/meteomod/)
- **Marsset et al. 2022, debiased MITHNEOS**: Debiased taxonomy fractions for each Granvik escape route from about 1,000 NEO spectra, with per-object escape-route probabilities tabulated. Overlap: Gives P(taxonomy | escape route). Shows nu6 and 3:1J are compositionally the same, which removes the leverage for NHATS. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13796)
- **MANOS: Devogele et al. 2019 and Moskovitz et al. 2026**: Spectra of 210 and colours of 189 small, low delta-v NEOs; documented S+Q declining and X rising with H; tested and rejected source region as the cause. Overlap: Direct measurements on the NHATS-type population, including seven objects below 4 km/s. The right source for base rates. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15872)
- **Lopez-Oquendo et al. 2026, UKIRT small-NEO survey**: Probabilistic taxonomy for 122 NEOs of 5-150 m with a first-order debiasing; compared METEOMOD and NEOMOD family assignments. Overlap: Size-matched base rates and a published demonstration that the family models disagree per object. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28779)
- **Binzel et al. 2019, MITHNEOS**: Taxonomy for more than 1,000 NEOs correlated with escape regions and delta-v. Overlap: States that the delta-V <= 7 km/s subset is a representative mix of all NEOs. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05090)
- **Granvik & Brown 2018; Jenniskens & Devillepoix 2025**: Escape-route probabilities for 25 falls with orbits; later review linking 75-81 falls to families using orbits and exposure ages. Overlap: The orbit -> material mapping at metre scale has been attempted on the only labelled set that exists; family-level answers conflict with Broz/Marsset for L chondrites. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.07229)
- **Jedicke et al. 2025; Alessi & Jedicke 2026; Fenucci et al. 2026**: Steady-state numbers of lunar-origin minimoons and co-orbitals versus main-belt ones, and a specific test for Kamo'oalewa. Overlap: Provides the only quantitative basis for a lunar-ejecta prior on Earth-like orbits, with very wide uncertainty. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13296)
- **Dotson et al. Bayesian property-inference network (NASA Ames, used in PDC 2025)**: Infers taxonomy, density, porosity and strength for a single object from albedo and population priors. Overlap: Closest existing analogue to the project's fusion step, built for planetary-defence scenarios rather than a catalogue. I confirmed it exists but did not read the paper. (https://cneos.jpl.nasa.gov/pd/cs/pdc25/PDC25-Epoch2-physical_properties.pdf)
- **Broz et al. 2024 (Nature and A&A) and Marsset et al. 2024 (Nature)**: Collisional and transport modelling of 38+ families to predict meteorite-class and NEO-class shares at 1 m and 1 km. Overlap: Supplies the size-dependent family mix that any source prior would lean on; metre-scale shares are model extrapolations. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08552)

### Opportunities

- **Publish the quantified null: for every NHATS object show the Granvik, NEOMOD and METEOMOD source vectors side by side, with the measured information content (0.003-0.005 bits) and the hold-out skill test, pre-registered.** New: I found no published out-of-sample calibration test of any orbit -> composition predictor, and no statement of how little the orbit says for the accessible population specifically. Feasibility: High. All three tables are public; I reproduced the core numbers in one session with about 300 lines of Python. Risk: It is a negative result; reviewers may call it obvious given Binzel 2019 and Moskovitz 2026. Licences are absent on two of the three tables, so redistribution of derived per-object values needs the authors' consent.
- **Origin-mixture layer for the Earth-like corner: P(main belt / lunar ejecta / artificial) as a function of a, e, i, size and arc, with dated predictions (Kamo'oalewa before the November 2027 sample return; low delta-v objects with apparitions in 2028-2032).** New: The source models contain no lunar or tidal source and already miss observed counts there; Alessi & Jedicke give population numbers but no per-object probabilities. Feasibility: Medium. Needs the Jedicke/Alessi ejecta orbital distributions (figures and text are public; the synthetic populations may need a request) and a hierarchical fit to fewer than ten labelled objects. Risk: The lunar rate is uncertain by about five orders of magnitude, so the posterior will be dominated by the hyperprior and by two or three spectra.
- **Pooled H-binned base-rate table with between-survey variance, built from MANOS spectra and colours, UKIRT, NEOSHIELD-2, Sanchez and MITHNEOS, stated explicitly as H-conditioned, with the joint class-albedo-diameter posterior derived from it.** New: Each survey publishes its own fractions; nobody has published a pooled, H-conditioned prior with survey disagreement treated as uncertainty, and the H-versus-diameter distinction is routinely blurred. Feasibility: High for the pooling (tables are in the papers); medium for harmonising taxonomic systems (Sanchez's Sx class, colour-based vs spectral classes). Risk: Survey target selection is not random within an H bin, and classification systems differ enough that pooled fractions can shift by 20 percentage points.
- **Resolve the X-complex: test Granvik's 21% Hungaria share against NEOMOD's 0.7% using existing albedos and colours of small low delta-v NEOs, since Hungaria-derived objects should be high-albedo E-types.** New: The two leading models disagree by a factor of about 30 on this for NHATS orbits, and the composition of the 20-25% X-complex is where any metal-rich objects sit. Feasibility: Medium. Depends on how many small X-complex NEOs have thermal albedos; NEO Surveyor will help later. Risk: Too few small X-types with albedos today (Moskovitz has 8 X-types at H > 27) to separate E, M and P.
- **Metre-scale external check: compare predicted class mix for Earth impactors with the 61 falls and 13 imminent impactors under an explicit, uncertain survival model.** New: Impactors are selected by collision probability, which mimics the accessibility selection, so they probe NHATS-like orbits better than telescopic samples do. Feasibility: High for data assembly; medium for the survival model. Risk: The survival correction (factor 15-40 for carbonaceous material) is as uncertain as the thing being tested.

### Reasons it fails

- The two escape routes that carry 95% or more of NHATS source probability, nu6 and 3:1J, have statistically identical debiased compositions (S/Q 44.4% vs 43.3%), so the escape-route chain cannot separate objects.
- Low delta-v orbits are dynamically evolved and close-encounter-scattered; they have lost memory of the delivery resonance and family. Devogele 2019 says this explicitly for low-MOID objects.
- The published priors disagree on the base rate by an order of magnitude (5% to 67% dark or carbonaceous), so any single prior would look precise while being model-dependent.
- Mixing H-conditioned and diameter-conditioned fractions would bias every dark-class probability by a factor of several; debiased literature values are the wrong input for per-object priors.
- All source models are extrapolated at NHATS sizes: Granvik stops at H = 25 (74% of NHATS is fainter), NEOMOD at H = 28 (15% is fainter), NEOMOD3 albedos are extrapolated below 100 m from 428 detections, and METEOMOD maps are for 1 m bodies with about 10^3 particles per family.
- Family-level METEOMOD output on NHATS is partly sampling noise: its apparent information halves when maps are rebinned.
- The main-belt delivery models under-predict small NEOs on Earth-like orbits by up to about 30% and contain no lunar or tidal-disruption source, which is exactly where the cheapest targets are.
- Family-to-meteorite assignments are not settled: Jenniskens & Devillepoix put L chondrites at Hertha/Nysa where Marsset puts them at Massalia.
- Labelled sets are tiny or filtered: 61 falls, 4 typed imminent impactors, 23 NHATS objects with SBDB taxonomy, none below 5 km/s. Any trained orbit -> class model would overfit.
- Fireball strength classes are not composition: irons cannot be identified from strength, and the atmospheric and perihelion filters depend on orbit, which contaminates any orbit-class relation learned from falls.
- The X-complex (20-25% of small low delta-v NEOs) cannot be split into enstatite, metal and dark primitive material without albedo, so a 'metal-rich' prior from orbit alone is close to uninformed.
- A website that shows per-object composition probabilities derived mainly from this prior would mislead by displaying the same number for every object with false individuality.

### Prior art

- **SsODNet (Berthier, Carry, Mahlke, Normand 2023) with ssoCard, datacloud, ssoBFT and the rocks client**: Compiles published physical and dynamical parameters for ~1.2 million asteroids from over 3,000 articles and selects a best estimate per property with a bibliographic reference; includes taxonomy, albedo, colours, spins, density, thermal inertia, Yarkovsky, radar polarization, delta-v and source-region probabilities. Overlap: Very high. It is the fusion layer for channel 3 and partly channels 1 and 2. It covers 34 of the 37 NHATS objects under 6 km/s that have any composition data. It gives single best values, not calibrated probabilities, and nothing for objects with no measurement. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10697)
- **Sergeyev, Carry et al. 2023, Compositional properties of planet-crossing asteroids from astronomical surveys**: Merged SDSS, SkyMapper, Gaia and literature colours into one catalog and assigned probabilistic taxonomic complexes to 7,401 planet-crossers (2,277 true NEAs); explicitly classified 42 mission-accessible NEOs and predicted the taxonomic mix of source regions. Overlap: High. It is a survey-fusion, probabilistic NEA classification that already singles out mission-accessible objects. 211 of its objects are in NHATS today, 12 under 6 km/s. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03555)
- **MANOS (Thirouin+2016, 2018; Devogele+2019)**: Dedicated telescope survey of low delta-v small NEOs: 210 visible spectra with taxonomy (mean size ~60 m) and lightcurves for more than 140 objects. Overlap: Same target population by design, but with new observations. Reaches 96 NHATS objects with spectra, only 4 under 5 km/s. It is the main validation set at small sizes. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04788)
- **Mahlke, Carry and Mattei 2022 taxonomy and the classy tool**: Probabilistic taxonomy from 2,983 spectra plus albedo, classifying 4,526 asteroids with per-class probabilities; classy retrieves public spectra and classifies in several schemes. Overlap: Supplies the probabilistic classification machinery and class probabilities for 175 NHATS objects. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11229)
- **MITHNEOS (Binzel+2019; Marsset+2022)**: Visible and NIR spectra of more than 1,000 NEOs, debiased compositional distribution, and source-region links per class. Overlap: The reference set linking NEA spectral class to source region and meteorite type; mostly larger NEAs, with 34 of the Marsset objects in NHATS. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13796)
- **Spitzer ExploreNEOs, NEOSurvey and NEOLegacy (Trilling et al.; final compilation by Mommert)**: Diameters and albedos for 2,132 NEOs from 4.5 micron photometry. Overlap: Largest albedo set for small NEAs; 112 NHATS objects. Mostly absent from SsODNet, so it is a ready addition. (https://github.com/mommermi/spitzerneos)
- **Navarro-Meza et al. 2024, RATIR observations of 323 NEOs**: Multi-band colours and per-class probabilities (C+X, Q, S, D, V) for NEOs observed shortly after discovery. Overlap: Probabilistic classes for 24 NHATS objects; not ingested by SsODNet. (https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/viz-bin/asu-tsv?-source=J/AJ/167/163)
- **Lopez-Oquendo, Trilling, Mommert, Marsset 2026, Probing the Taxonomic Diversity of the Small Near-Earth Object Population**: Rapid-response UKIRT NIR spectrophotometry of 122 NEOs of 5-150 m with probabilistic classes and a bias-corrected population dominated by C/X bodies. Overlap: Directly addresses the composition mix of decametre NEOs with probabilistic outputs and debiasing; a comparison point and a possible validation set. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28779)
- **MP3C (Delbo et al., Observatoire de la Cote d'Azur)**: Second aggregator of asteroid physical properties with best values and a TAP service. Overlap: Independent cross-check of SsODNet for albedo, diameter and mass; nothing extra for NHATS. (https://mp3c.oca.eu/)
- **SRP and Yarkovsky densities for tiny NEAs (Micheli+2012/2013/2014, Mommert+2014, Farnocchia+2017, Bolin+2025)**: Derived area-to-mass ratios and bulk densities for 2009 BD, 2011 MD, 2012 LA, 2015 TC25 and 2024 PT5 from non-gravitational accelerations. Overlap: Channel 1 has been applied object by object; all five are NHATS objects under 6 km/s and already sit in SsODNet. A systematic pass over the 23 objects with A2 and 12 with A1 has not been published as a catalog. (https://ssp.imcce.fr/webservices/ssodnet/api/ssocard/2009_BD)
- **NASA Ames NEO Properties Database (v2018.5)**: Aggregated NEA taxonomy (~690), diameters (~2,300) and measured physical properties of over 900 meteorites for planetary defence. Overlap: An earlier NEA-specific aggregator with a meteorite property link; stale and unreachable today. (https://neoproperties.arc.nasa.gov/home/index)

### Opportunities

- **Publish a per-object NHATS evidence ledger: for all 7,226 objects, what is measured, by whom, with which method, and where sources disagree, refreshed on a schedule from SsODNet, SBDB, Spitzer, RATIR, LCDB and the radar API.** New: No public source reports physical coverage keyed on NHATS. SsODNet is not keyed on accessibility, lags two months, and misses Spitzer, RATIR and JPL A1/A2. The coverage table in this report did not exist before today. Feasibility: High. Everything was pulled and cross-matched in one session with about 3,000 API calls and a few hundred lines of Python. Risk: It is a catalog join, not science, unless paired with calibrated inference. Redistribution needs attribution because SsODNet, the Spitzer repo and LCDB state no licence.
- **Turn single best classes into class probabilities for the labelled NEAs by combining existing probabilistic outputs (Mahlke+2022 per-class probabilities, Sergeyev+2023 complex probabilities, RATIR and UKIRT class probabilities) and mapping them to the project's four classes.** New: SsODNet keeps only one class per object. The probabilistic tables exist separately on VizieR and have not been merged. Feasibility: Medium. Tables are small and public through the CfA VizieR mirror; the scheme mapping (Tholen, Bus, Bus-DeMeo, Mahlke to four classes) needs explicit rules. Risk: Metal-rich is not separable from other X-types without albedo, so that class will carry wide uncertainty for most objects.
- **Use the roughly 4,000 NEAs with composition data (about 455 inside NHATS) as a held-out test of orbit-only priors: predict class from orbit and source region alone, then score against the labels with pre-registered metrics.** New: Source-region priors exist (SsODNet carries them for H < 25) but a calibration test against per-object labels restricted to accessible orbits has not been published. Feasibility: High for H < 25; for H >= 25 (75% of NHATS) the Granvik-type probabilities are absent and would need extrapolation. Risk: Labelled objects are larger and brighter than the targets (median H 23.3 against 26.2), and small-NEO surveys report a different class mix, so calibration may not transfer.
- **Systematic non-gravitational pass over low delta-v objects: 23 objects under 6 km/s have A2 and 12 have A1 in SBDB, but only 5 have a published density, and 17 have no composition measurement at all.** New: Published SRP and Yarkovsky densities are one-object papers. A uniform treatment with stated priors on size, shape and thermal properties is missing. Feasibility: Medium. Inputs are public; the physics needs priors that dominate the answer. Risk: Results are bounds conditional on assumed size and albedo, and could be read as measurements.
- **Automated consistency checks across catalogs (H against diameter against albedo; class against albedo) to flag and report errors upstream.** New: Errors pass through aggregators unflagged; one was found by inspection today (2010 NU71, H 22.7, listed at 1.13 km). Feasibility: High. SsODNet has a feedback channel for submitting data. Risk: Low scientific weight on its own; useful mainly as data hygiene and credibility.

### Reasons it fails

- Ground truth is too thin where it matters: 9 classified objects among the 286 under 5 km/s and 24 among the 726 under 6 km/s. No calibration claim can be supported on that subset alone.
- The labelled set is biased toward larger, brighter, higher-albedo objects seen at favourable apparitions. A model validated on it will look better than it is for 10 m objects.
- SsODNet already does the catalog fusion. A project that only joins catalogs duplicates it and adds a few dozen objects from Spitzer and RATIR.
- Gaia DR3 contributes nothing to the target population (3 NHATS objects with spectra, none under 6 km/s), so a plan that leans on Gaia spectra for accessible objects is empty.
- Source-region priors in SsODNet exist only for H < 25, a quarter of NHATS. For the rest the prior has to be extrapolated beyond the model's validated range.
- Best-value albedos are computed from H, and H for H > 25 objects is uncertain by several tenths of a magnitude, so albedo-based class separation (needed for metal-rich) is weak.
- All small-object densities are model-dependent (SRP or Yarkovsky plus assumed size and thermal properties). Presenting them as measurements would mislead.
- Mixed taxonomic schemes and photometric classes with large error (467 of 2,279 Sergeyev NEAs are unclassified) make a clean four-class label set hard to build.
- Upstreams are stale or fragile: LCDB frozen since October 2023, NEOCC physical data without a curator since 2018, PDS NEOWISE table stops at year 3, and six services were down, blocked or throttled on the day of the check.
- Licences are implicit for SsODNet, MP3C, LCDB, the Spitzer table and Johnston's archive; JPL forbids simultaneous API requests and embedding its APIs in a website. A public fused table needs attribution and its own hosted snapshot.
- The all-NHATS and all-NEA SsODNet numbers come from random samples (1,758 and 600 objects), not the full table, so they carry sampling error of about 15-25%.

## new-surveys

### Prior art

- **Sergeyev et al. 2023, 'Compositional properties of planet-crossing asteroids from astronomical surveys' (A&A 679, A148)**: Combined SDSS, SkyMapper, Gaia DR3 reflectance spectra and ground compilations into taxonomic classes for 7,401 NEOs, with updated SDSS photometry for 470 NEOs, 42 mission-accessible NEOs classified, and source-region predictions. Overlap: Direct prior art for fusing archival colours and assigning taxonomy plus source region to NEOs. The multi-band archives (SDSS, SkyMapper, Gaia) are already mined; the project must start from this catalogue, not redo it. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03555)
- **Moskovitz et al. 2026, 'NEO Colors from the Mission Accessible Near-Earth Object Survey (MANOS)'**: griz colours for 189 NEOs; found the S-complex fraction falls from about 65% at km scale to about one third below 50 m while X-complex rises; showed that uncorrected light-curve variation distorts non-simultaneous colours. Overlap: Targeted survey of exactly the mission-accessible population; sets the size-dependent taxonomy prior and warns about the main systematic in using Rubin's non-simultaneous colours. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15872)

### Opportunities

- **Rubin colours for mission-accessible NEAs from public MPC ADES records (station X05), refreshed continuously, with a pre-registered light-curve-scatter model for non-simultaneous filters. Pilot on 2025 GH1 (H 25.5, 102 points in u, g, r, i) and (847431).** New: No NEA-composition analysis of Rubin photometry was found; published Rubin colour work is on main-belt objects. Rubin is the only survey deep enough to reach decameter NEAs routinely. Feasibility: High. The MPC API returns filter, magnitude and rmsMag; 1,000 objects took about 12 minutes with four workers, so all 7,226 NHATS objects take about 1.5 hours. Data are kilobytes per object. About 35 NHATS objects have Rubin data now (0.5% of sample). Risk: Small numbers until Rubin runs steadily (three weeks of survey observing so far, MPC submissions still ad hoc); fast rotators make non-simultaneous colours unreliable; visible colours give S/C/X/V only and nothing on water.
- **NEOWISE thermal fits (diameter, albedo) for NHATS and other NEAs that have NEOWISE detections but no published size, using the final 2024 release, then feed diameters into the Yarkovsky density bound.** New: 15 of 20 sampled NHATS objects with NEOWISE astrometry have no diameter in SBDB; no publication of fits for reactivation years 8-11 was found. Feasibility: High. About 145 NHATS objects have NEOWISE astrometry; IRSA MOST and the single-exposure source table serve per-object queries; NEATM code is open source (ATM, kete). Risk: Some of these fits may already be published but absent from SBDB; two-band fits carry 20-40% diameter errors and depend on an assumed beaming parameter; Myers et al. found about half the detections must be discarded.

MOST lookup and 4 MB cutouts were tested today; a few GB per object; photometry on 6.2 arcsec pixels with supplied ePSF and per-pixel wavelength. Fits on one workstation. Feasibility: Medium. 4.6% of NHATS objects have 10+ ATLAS points and 1.1% have 10+ ZTF points; forced photometry costs about 20 minutes per object. ### Reasons it fails

- The typical NHATS object was visible for about two weeks (median arc 12.2 days, median 37 observations). No archive release can add data for an object no survey pointed at while it was bright, so for roughly 95% of the list the 'new survey' channel contributes nothing and the posterior equals the prior.
- SPHEREx does not reach small NEAs: 0 of 328 random NHATS objects were brighter than predicted V=18 in any covering frame and 1 of 328 brighter than V=19, against a per-channel depth near AB 18.8. Advertising a 3-micron water channel for mission-accessible objects would mislead.
- Even for the larger NEAs SPHEREx can see, thermal emission dominates below 3 micron near 1 au, so the 2.7-3.1 micron hydration band sits on a steep thermal slope; a small error in the thermal model creates or erases a band.
- SPHEREx spectra are built from hundreds of exposures over about two weeks at different rotation phases and phase angles. Without light curves the spectrum shape is contaminated by rotation; the science team built a dedicated telescope (SOLO) to address this.
- Rubin colours are non-simultaneous and small NEAs often rotate in minutes with large amplitudes; MANOS showed uncorrected light-curve variation changes individual classifications and biases ensembles.
- Rubin's data flow is fragile on the project's timescale: the LSST started 2026-06-29, the observatory was offline 2026-07-15 to 2026-10-27, the camera was down again on 15-17 November, MPC submissions are ad hoc, and DR1 is not expected until July-November 2028.
- Gaia DR4 contains almost no mission-accessible objects (8 NHATS objects in DR3, faintest NEA H 20.3) and its solar-system results will be published by DPAC on release day; it helps the prior, not the per-object evidence.
- The multi-band archives have been mined already (7,401 NEOs classified by Sergeyev et al. 2023) and the measured yield of further archival serendipity is about 0.05 detections per NEO; discovery surveys that do cover NHATS objects (Catalina, Pan-STARRS w band) are single-band.
- The size-dependent taxonomy prior for decameter NEOs is contested in current literature (S fraction about one third below 50 m in MANOS; CX-dominated after debiasing in the UKIRT survey; 66% S below 300 m in Sanchez et al. 2024), so a catalogue that is mostly prior inherits that disagreement.
- NEOWISE 'missing diameters' may be an SBDB indexing gap, not a science gap; and two-band thermal fits for small NEAs have large systematic errors that Myers et al. documented.
- MPC-reported magnitudes outside Rubin are heterogeneous (unfiltered data tied to Gaia G, catalogue-band labels), so a census of 'bands present' overstates usable colour information.
- Official products will overtake home-made ones: SPHEREx Solar System catalog (May 2028), Rubin DR1 (2028), NEO Surveyor (launch no earlier than September 2027). A catalogue that cannot ingest them will date quickly.

## industry

**Headline.** Remote characterization is not what holds asteroid mining back today (money, spacecraft reliability and launch slips are), but miners do lack size, spin and class data for their candidate lists, and no public catalog gives calibrated per-object probabilities. The plan must build on Karman+'s open-source Prospector (Sept 2026), which already fuses the sparse public data into deterministic tiers and leaves every uncharacterized object as "unknown".

WHAT MINERS ASKED. ASIME 2016 (arXiv:1612.00709; 30 questions from Planetary Resources, DSI, TransAstra) and ASIME 2018 (arXiv:1904.11831; 21 composition questions) asked: which properties must be known (Galache's order: size, composition, mass/density, spin, interior, surface, accurate orbit); how to speed spectral characterization below 300 m; whether orbit or shape predicts composition (orbit: only statistically via source region); how to tell rubble pile from monolith; how to detect hydration (0.7 µm band is the practical proxy for 3.1 µm); how far spectral class predicts bulk composition (Michel: "I don't think the current confidence is high"). Two of the six knowledge gaps are this project: a map of "low delta-v, low synodic period and low-albedo NEOs", and testing whether dynamically predicted source regions match physical data. The miners themselves were lukewarm on prospecting: Bonin (DSI) said "a priori immaculate knowledge is going to take too long"; Sercel (TransAstra) said the paradigm is "FISHING, not prospecting".

PRACTICAL ORDER OF PROPERTIES (my synthesis): usable orbit; reachability in the launch window (delta-v, flight time, synodic period, comms distance); size; spin state; composition class and hydration; structure; binarity; grade. NHATS today has 7,226 objects; 69.8% have orbit condition code ≥6. The 286 with delta-v ≤5 km/s have median H 27.7 (roughly 5–22 m); only 2 could exceed 100 m. Alarcón et al. 2026 find ≳94% of H>26 objects are fast rotators.

VALUE. Avoid dollars. Use (1) mining resource-confidence classes (Inferred/Indicated/Measured; Lewicki et al. 2021), (2) distributions of tonnes of water and metal, (3) Elvis's P_ore = P_type × P_rich × P_acc × P_eng, (4) probes saved (Elvis & Esty: 22 assay probes at P_rich 0.1, 4 at 0.5, for 90% confidence), (5) mass returned per mass launched. Best numbers: iron-meteorite total PGM 14.7 / 40.8 / 107.9 ppm at 10th/50th/90th percentile versus 1.07 / 3.86 / 7.36 ppm for terrestrial deposits, maximum ~230 ppm, and "no known way to remotely identify" grade (Cannon, Gialich & Acain 2023). Water: Ivuna (CI) 12.73 wt%, Ryugu 6.84 wt%, Bennu 0.84–0.95 wt% H (≤ ~8 wt% water-equivalent). Elvis assumed 20 wt%. Techno-economic studies (Hein 2020, Colvin 2020, Sonter 1997) put in-space water before Earth-return PGMs; figures of merit are expected NPV, mass payback ratio, break-even time and throughput rate.

EXISTING TOOLS. Asterank: mass × class composition × price, flat cost percentage, no uncertainty. NHATS: accessibility only. NEOfixer and the ESA NEOCC priority list (being retired in favour of NEOfixer): astrometric follow-up for planetary defence. MANOS: observations of low-delta-v NEOs (228 lightcurves; 189 colours). Karman+ Prospector (github.com/Karmanplus/prospector, Apache-2.0): merges SsODNet, Lowell astorb, Ondřejov periods and MANOS spectra; adds expected minimum perihelion (Toliou et al. 2021) and P(period > critical); outputs tiers S/A/B/C/D; "Unknowns never promote"; ships "no calibrated inputs". No probabilistic, validated per-object prospect catalog is public.

COMPANIES, 20 Nov 2026. AstroForge: Vestri, renamed DeepSpace-2, has not launched; it rides with IM-3, now Q1 2027. 200 kg, ~$10M. In June 2026 it had "about 28 candidate asteroids" and no fixed target. Its 2025 target 2022 OB5 was chosen for being "not an S-type" and within 2 million km at flyby; described as "up to 100 meters"; JPL gives H 28.99 (2.8–12 m), and GTC measured a 1.542-minute rotation and X-complex colour (arXiv:2605.04784, "Accessible does not mean exploitable"). Karman+: $20M seed; first mission slipped to mid-2028 and now targets Apophis (S-type) as a tech demo; says rotation is "one of the bigger unknowns", known for ~10–15% of objects; wants slow-rotating C-type rubble piles for return missions. TransAstra: 10-m capture bag ground-tested 16 Nov 2026; ~250 candidate small NEAs; first capture 2028 at earliest. Asteroid Mining Corporation and Origin Space: no 2026 news found. SpaceX's S-1 names asteroid mining as a future market that does "not currently exist".

GROUND TRUTH. Tianwen-2 is at Kamoʻoalewa (since July 2026; sample return Nov 2027); JWST gives 18 ± 2 m, albedo ~0.36–0.59, 27.9-min rotation, against earlier 36–100 m and "lunar" readings. Hayabusa2 flew past Torifune on 5 July 2026 (contact binary); the 1998 KY26 rendezvous was abandoned on 16–19 Nov 2026 after engine failure, replaced by a 2015 BM510 flyby in April 2028. Hera reached Didymos in early November 2026. Psyche arrives Aug 2029; Ramses and OSIRIS-APEX reach Apophis in 2029; DESTINY+ launches FY2028; NEO Surveyor launches no earlier than Sept 2027.

USERS. Observers allocating telescope time; small mission teams with candidate lists; scientists; journalists and investors checking claims. Agency demand is weak: SBAG's ISRU goal "was planned, but interest dwindled". Publish in Prospector's enrichment schema plus class probabilities, quantiles, provenance, and next observing window.

### Prior art

- **Karman+ Prospector (open source, Sept 2026) and the private Karman+ Asteroid Leaderboard**: Screens the SBDB catalogue by delta-v, then merges SsODNet, Lowell astorb, Ondrejov periods and MANOS spectra per target; adds class-median albedo sizing, expected minimum perihelion (Toliou et al. 2021), a size-conditioned spin distribution and cohesion; assigns a deterministic tier S/A/B/C/D. Overlap: High for channel 3 (fusing sparse existing data) and for the spin and thermal-history priors. No class probabilities, no source-region composition prior, no non-gravitational density, no validation against known answers, and no published output table. Its enrichment schema is a ready-made delivery format. (https://github.com/Karmanplus/prospector)
- **Alarcon et al. 2026, 'Accessible does not mean exploitable: HiPERCAM reveals the ultra-fast rotation of 2022 OB5' (Icarus)**: First characterization of the first commercial prospecting target: 1.542-min rotation, sub-10 m, X-complex; shows fast rotation dominates at the sizes and delta-v values favoured for missions. Overlap: Makes the project's core argument for one object and supplies a known-answer case. Shows spin must be a first-class field. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04784)
- **ASIME 2016 and ASIME 2018 white papers (Graps et al.)**: Recorded miners' questions and scientists' answers; defined six science knowledge gaps, including an accessibility/albedo map and testing source region against physical properties. Overlap: Defines the requirement the project answers (SKG 4 and 5). Also records miners' scepticism about waiting for characterization. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00709)
- **Elvis 2014 and Elvis & Esty 2014**: Factorized the probability an NEO is ore-bearing and computed the number of assay probes needed as a function of P_rich. Overlap: Provides the non-dollar value framework and the metric (probes saved) by which a catalog's worth can be stated. Population-level only. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.4450)
- **Cannon, Gialich & Acain 2023, 'Precious and structural metals on asteroids'**: Recompiled PGM grades in iron meteorites and metal fractions by meteorite class against terrestrial ore; corrected Kargel 1994 and Elvis 2014. Overlap: Supplies the PGM grade distribution and the statement that grade cannot be sensed remotely, which limits what a metal 'prospect' can claim. (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pss.2022.105608)
- **Jedicke et al. 2018; Rivkin & DeMeo 2019; Sanchez & McInnes 2011**: Population estimates: water-bearing NEOs versus return-trip delta-v; hydrated NEO counts from the Ch class; accessible NEA mass versus transfer energy. Overlap: Channel 2 at population level (source region to taxonomy to water). None gives per-object posteriors or validation. (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pss.2018.04.005)
- **Hein, Matheson & Fries 2020; Calla et al. 2018; Andrews et al. 2015; Sonter 1997; Colvin et al. 2020; Metzger 2023**: Techno-economic models: NPV, mass payback ratio, break-even time, throughput rate, delivered cost per kg; water before PGMs. Overlap: Defines the figures of merit the catalog's value block should feed. Shows target composition enters only as one multiplier (resource fraction). (https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03836)
- **Asterank (Ian Webster; acquired by Planetary Resources 2013)**: Public ranking of asteroids by dollar value and profit from mass, spectral class and market price. Overlap: The discredited predecessor. Same ambition (per-object ranking), no uncertainty, no validation. (https://www.asterank.com/about)
- **JPL NHATS**: Daily list of mission-accessible NEAs with delta-v, duration, size range from H, orbit condition code and next observing windows. Overlap: The project's object list and its accessibility and observability fields. No physical characterization. (https://cneos.jpl.nasa.gov/nhats/intro.html)
- **MANOS (Mission Accessible Near-Earth Object Survey, Lowell)**: Lightcurves, spectra and colours for low-delta-v sub-km NEOs; estimated ~1,230 NEOs must be characterized to find 100 viable mission targets. Overlap: The main observational data and validation set for small accessible NEOs; already an input to Prospector. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15872)
- **NEOfixer (Catalina Sky Survey) and ESA NEOCC Priority List**: Rank NEOs for astrometric follow-up by planetary-defence importance, benefit, cost and urgency. Overlap: Covers orbit-quality triage. Could carry a characterization-value term but does not. (https://neofixer.arizona.edu/faq/how-does-neofixer-assign-priority)
- **Lewicki, Graps, Elvis, Metzger & Rivkin 2021 decadal white paper**: Mapped asteroid resource knowledge onto mining feasibility stages and recommended open, interoperable asteroid property databases. Overlap: States the need for the catalog and supplies the resource-confidence vocabulary. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02435)
- **SpaceMiningData.com**: Independent, CC BY directory of space-mining missions, organisations and the asteroids they name, with JSON/CSV downloads. Overlap: Adjacent: tracks who targets what. Not a prospect ranking. (https://spaceminingdata.com/missions/astroforge-odin.html)

### Opportunities

- **Publish the catalog as a drop-in Prospector enrichment table (same column names) extended with class probabilities, quantiles and provenance, so the uncharacterized 'tier C' majority gets a calibrated prior instead of 'unknown'.** New: Prospector is public but deterministic, treats missing data as unknown, and ships no calibrated inputs or output list. No one publishes the table. Feasibility: High. Schema and data sources are public (SsODNet, astorb, Ondrejov, MANOS, SBDB, NHATS). Mostly engineering plus the prior models. Risk: Karman+ may not adopt it, and other companies keep their inputs private, so the first user is not guaranteed.
- **Pre-register dated predictions for objects about to get ground truth: Kamo'oalewa (density, sample class; capsule Nov 2027), Didymos/Dimorphos (Hera, 2027), 2015 BM510 (flyby April 2028), the DeepSpace-2 target, Apophis (2029), Psyche (2029).** New: No public, scored forecasts of small-body properties exist; Kamo'oalewa's class is still disputed (lunar, LL chondrite, enstatite). Feasibility: High. Requires frozen snapshots and a scoring rule; Tianwen-2 is on site now. Risk: Few objects and slow feedback; a handful of outcomes cannot establish calibration, and CNSA or companies may not release data.
- **Rank objects by the value of the next observation: which single measurement (lightcurve, albedo, 0.7 micron band, radar) most reduces uncertainty, and when the object is next observable.** New: NHATS gives windows and NEOfixer ranks astrometry, but nothing ranks characterization value. This serves observers, the most concrete user group found. Feasibility: Medium. Needs an information-gain model on top of the catalog; windows come from NHATS. Risk: Observers may not trust or use an outside priority list; faint targets need 8-10 m telescopes with scarce time.
- **Replace dollar value with a per-object block: resource-confidence class (Inferred/Indicated/Measured), tonnes of water and metal as distributions, Elvis factors with intervals, and assay probes saved for a shortlist versus a blind draw.** New: Avoids Asterank's failure while using vocabulary miners and investors already know; the probes-saved metric ties directly to mission cost ($3.5M to $30M per probe). Feasibility: High. Concentration priors exist (Cannon 2023; Ryugu and Bennu samples). Risk: Media will still convert tonnes into dollars; PGM figures remain unconstrained and may look empty.
- **A public audit page of company target claims against public data, starting with 2022 OB5 ('up to 100 m' versus H = 28.99 and a 1.5-min spin) and Kamo'oalewa.** New: The 2022 OB5 size claim stood for a year though H alone contradicted it. No neutral checker exists. Feasibility: High. A few objects, all with public data. Risk: Adversarial framing could close doors with the companies who are the catalog's potential users.
- **Report the null result honestly: how many NHATS objects survive orbit quality, size, spin and class filters at stated probability.** New: Published counts (Elvis, Jedicke) predate the current list and ignore spin and orbit quality together. Feasibility: High once the catalog exists. Risk: The answer may be near zero for surface operations, which is scientifically useful but makes a weak 'prospect' product.

### Reasons it fails

- Early missions pick targets on launch date and communications distance, not composition (AstroForge 2022 OB5; Karman+ Apophis), so the catalog may change no decision before about 2028.
- The real value drivers (PGM grade, water weight fraction, regolith mechanics) cannot be sensed remotely; AstroForge's own paper says PGM grade has no remote indicator, and X-complex objects are degenerate between metal, enstatite and primitive types without albedo.
- The accessible population is tiny bodies (median H 27.7 for delta-v <= 5 km/s); at least 94% of H > 26 objects are fast rotators and about 70% of NHATS objects have poor orbits, so few targets are workable whatever their composition.
- Known answers are mostly bodies above 300 m. For decametre objects there is Kamo'oalewa and 2022 OB5, and Hayabusa2's 1998 KY26 rendezvous was cancelled in November 2026. Calibration shown on large bodies does not transfer to the NHATS population.
- Even the best-observed small NEA was wrong by a factor of 2-5 in size and is still disputed in class (Kamo'oalewa). Stated uncertainties that would not have covered that case are miscalibrated.
- Karman+ has already open-sourced the data-fusion pipeline and MANOS holds the spectra; if the project's priors and density channel add little skill, it duplicates existing work.
- Any value figure will be quoted without caveats, as Asterank's trillions were.
- There is no paying customer: SpaceX's S-1 says the market does not currently exist, techno-economic studies need over 100 spacecraft to break even on water, and SBAG dropped its ISRU goal for lack of community interest.
- Meteorite-to-asteroid links for C and X types are weak (ASIME 2018), and returned samples hold about 7 wt% water against the 20 wt% older ore estimates assumed, so 'carbonaceous-hydrated' overstates resource content unless hydration and thermal history are modelled.
- Secondary sources are unreliable on dates and sizes; several search summaries today carried stale or wrong launch dates. An agent-built catalog that ingests them without primary-source provenance will mislead.

## ground-truth

**Headline.** Independent truth for NEAs is about 35 to 40 objects (about a dozen with known composition, about 9 usable for checking Yarkovsky densities, none NHATS-sized except Kamo'oalewa, pending), so the gold set can only falsify the catalog, not calibrate it. Calibration has to come from a sealed, size-stratified silver hold-out, end-to-end synthetic and "degrade a known object" tests, and pre-registered predictions scored by 2027 to 2031 mission results.

GOLD TRUTH IS SMALL. Composition-grade truth is about a dozen NEAs: Itokawa (LL chondrite sample, 1.9 g/cm3), Ryugu (CI-like sample, 1.19), Bennu (hydrated carbonaceous sample, 1.19), Eros (L/LL from orbit, 2.67), Didymos (S-type, 2.60 +/- 0.14; Dimorphos 1.54 +/- 0.22, model-dependent), Toutatis (L-like, no mass), plus four pre-impact asteroids with recovered meteorites: 2008 TC3 (ureilite), 2018 LA (howardite), 2023 CX1 (L5-6), 2024 BX1 (aubrite). Only 2008 TC3 has both a pre-impact spectrum and a meteorite. The complete pre-impact list is 13 (https://neo.ssa.esa.int/past-impactors): 2008 TC3, 2014 AA, 2018 LA, 2019 MO, 2022 EB5, 2022 WJ1, 2023 CX1, 2024 BX1, 2024 RW1, 2024 UQ, 2024 XA1, 2026 JN4 (15 May 2026, no instrumental confirmation), 2026 RW1 (6 Sep 2026, first Aten); none in 2025. Direct bulk densities exist for about 15 to 20 NEAs (5 spacecraft plus radar and lightcurve binaries; Johnston lists 113 NEA systems), all larger than 300 m.

2026 UPDATES. Tianwen-2 has been at Kamo'oalewa since June/July 2026; JWST gives 15 to 21 m and a less-red, LL-like spectrum (arXiv 2606.24017); sample due November 2027. Hayabusa2 flew past Torifune on 5 July 2026; no composition result yet. Hera reached Didymos by 11 November 2026; Dimorphos mass expected 2027. Two NHATS mission targets shrank when checked: 1998 KY26 from 30 m to 11 m (albedo about 0.5), Kamo'oalewa from a 68x46x39 m model to about 20 m. H-based sizes of tiny NEAs were wrong by 2 to 3 times in both cases.

TARGET COVERAGE, measured today from JPL APIs. NHATS: 7,226 objects, 7,018 with H >= 22, median H 26.2, 286 with delta-v under 5 km/s. SBDB has taxonomy for 23, albedo 69, A2 169 (154 at 3 sigma or better), A1 18, rotation period 306. At least 226 are radar-detected (JPL lists 1,149 radar NEAs). SsODNet has about 220 with a taxonomic class, about 95 at H >= 22 (scaled from 477 ssoCards). All NEAs in SBDB: 43,151; taxonomy 327 (median H 17.9); albedo 1,205 (median H 19.1); A2 618 (573 at 3 sigma, 351 at 5 sigma); A1 21. Labelled sets sit 7 to 8 magnitudes brighter than the targets.

LABEL SETS. MITHNEOS (976 NEOs analysed; S+Q 66% observed, 42% after debiasing; carbonaceous 23% to 48%; arXiv 2202.13796). MANOS (210 spectra, mean H 25; arXiv 1909.04788). NEOShield-2 (147). NEOROCKS (93). Sanchez 2024 (84 under 300 m). Lopez-Oquendo 2026 (122 NEOs at 5 to 150 m, debiased population dark-dominated; arXiv 2608.28779). NEOWISE (1,845 NEOs through 2020). Spitzer (about 2,000). Gaia DR3 has only 104 NEAs, 3 in NHATS. Meteorite falls with orbits: 61 on Meier's list (77% ordinary chondrite, 7 carbonaceous, 1 iron), machine-readable at https://www.meteoriteorbits.info/wp-json/wp/v2/pages/5; 75 in Jenniskens and Devillepoix 2025. Fireballs: 824 European Network events with strength classes (CDS J/A+A/667/A157); CNEOS 1,074 events, 363 with velocity vectors, 9 of them internally inconsistent.

WHAT CANNOT BE VALIDATED ON NEAs. Metal-rich: one radar-confirmed NEA (1986 DA). Volatiles from non-gravitational residuals: 14 dark comets, none with detected gas; 1998 KY26's out-of-plane term fell from 4.2 sigma to 1.5 sigma. Hydration: 3-micron detections on NEAs are on S-types (8 of 29, 4 of 15); real truth is Bennu, Ryugu and 7 carbonaceous falls. SRP objects (21 with A1) have no independent density. Catalog densities mix direct and Yarkovsky-derived values: SsODNet gives Toutatis 0.73 and 2002 CE26 3.06 (radar: 0.9).

STATISTICS. Twelve gold objects detect "claims 90%, delivers 60%" with 77% power. Reliability bins need 30 to 50 objects each. A 0.10-bit log-score gain needs about 150 test objects at 2 sigma, 300 at 3 sigma.

PROTOCOL. Gold is a final exam only. Calibrate on silver labels with a sealed hold-out: split by object and dynamical cluster, mask the label's own channel, stratify by H, importance-weight toward the NHATS distribution and report effective N. Never use Yarkovsky-derived densities or H-derived sizes as truth. Marsset's per-source fractions are in-sample for MITHNEOS. Add an end-to-end synthetic population pushed through real selection (NEOMOD3), degrade-a-known-object tests (cut Bennu, Itokawa, Apophis arcs to NHATS-like data), negative controls (artificial objects, large bodies), a published hash of the test set and thresholds, and prospective predictions for Kamo'oalewa (2027), Dimorphos (2027), Apophis (2029), 1998 KY26 (2031), new impactors and new spectra.

MINIMUM BAR. Class channel: at least 0.10 bits per object over the debiased size-binned prior on at least 150 sealed H >= 22 objects, interval excluding zero, reliability within 0.10 per bin, no gold object's true class below 5%. Yarkovsky density: reproduce JPL A2 for 90% of the 351 strong detections, pass degraded-arc coverage, and at most one violation of the 95% upper bound among the 8 or 9 objects with independent density (Itokawa, Ryugu, Bennu, Didymos, Moshup, 2000 DP107, 1996 FG3, 2003 YT1, Apollo). SRP: recover 8 published A1 values and flag artificial objects. Dark-comet channel: anomaly flag only.

### Prior art

- **Marsset et al. 2022, debiased compositional distribution of MITHNEOS (AJ)**: Albedo-debiased taxonomy fractions for 976 NEOs, overall and per escape route (Granvik 2018 probabilities); validated by matching main-belt fractions near each route. Overlap: This is the orbit-to-source-to-class prior (channel 2) at population level. It is in-sample for MITHNEOS objects, limited to H < 23, and ignores targeting bias. The project would be applying it per object at H about 26. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13796)
- **Dorsey and Granvik 2026, source regions for NEOs sharing a taxonomic class (EPSC 2026 abstract)**: Builds an orbital model per taxonomic class with selection effects from the Granvik 2018 model; states that individual NEOs cannot be traced to a source and that per-object estimates over-weight dominant sources. Overlap: Direct overlap with channel 2, by the group that owns the NEO model. Their stated caveat is the main scientific objection to per-object source priors. (https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EPSC2026/EPSC2026-1111.html)
- **SsODNet / ssoBFT (IMCCE)**: Aggregates best-value taxonomy, albedo, diameter, density, Yarkovsky, colours, thermal inertia, source-region probabilities and delta-v for every asteroid, with provenance. Overlap: Already the fused catalog for channel 3 and already publishes per-object source-region probabilities. It has no calibrated class probabilities, no validation, and mixes model-dependent values with direct ones. (https://ssp.imcce.fr/data/ssoBFT-latest_Asteroid.parquet)
- **Dziadura et al. 2023 and 2025, Yarkovsky effect and bulk density of NEAs from Gaia DR3 / FPR**: Orbit fits for 446 NEAs; 49 reliable A2; bulk densities from population assumptions on thermal inertia and obliquity. Overlap: Channel 1 (Yarkovsky to density) done for about 50 objects, without validation against independent truth; its Toutatis and 1950 DA values disagree with other evidence. (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...680A..77D)
- **Chesley et al. 2014, Bennu density from Yarkovsky**: Published 1260 +/- 70 kg/m3 before OSIRIS-REx measured 1190. Overlap: The template for prospective, scored predictions; also the single clean success of channel 1. (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...680A..77D)
- **Mahlke, Carry and Mattei 2022, asteroid taxonomy from spectra and albedo**: Probabilistic 17-class taxonomy that handles partial wavelength coverage and missing albedo; 4,526 asteroids classified. Overlap: The existing probabilistic classifier for sparse data (channel 3). It uses albedo as an input, so it leaks if albedo is also a feature; it was not validated against held-out truth. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11229)
- **MANOS (Devogele et al. 2019) and Lopez-Oquendo et al. 2026**: Spectral or colour classes for 210 and 122 small, mission-accessible or newly discovered NEOs, with size-dependent and debiased class fractions. Overlap: These are the nearest thing to a labelled sample of the NHATS population and the natural sealed test set. Lopez-Oquendo already outputs probabilistic classes and a debiased small-NEO mix. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28779)
- **Seligman et al. 2023, 2024, dark comets**: Identified 14 inactive-looking NEOs with non-gravitational accelerations not explained by radiation; proposed outgassing. Overlap: This is the volatile-indicator channel. No positive has been confirmed and the flagship case has weakened. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07603)
- **Granvik and Brown 2018; Jenniskens and Devillepoix 2025**: Source-region probabilities for 25 meteorite falls; review of 75 falls with trajectories and proposed parent families. Overlap: Orbit-to-source applied to objects whose composition is known. Usable as a small test set for channel 2, with the atmospheric-survival bias as a caveat. (https://dfn.gfo.rocks/documents/papers_reprints/Jenniskens_2025_review_meteorite_sources.pdf)
- **Chow et al. 2026, imminent Earth impactors and LSST**: Tabulated the 11 pre-2026 imminent impactors and simulated Rubin's yield and selection biases. Overlap: Authoritative table for class (b) truth and a quantified description of its hemisphere and albedo biases. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05587)
- **Kareta et al. 2024, telescope-to-fireball characterisation of 2022 WJ1**: Closed the loop between pre-impact colours and fireball-derived strength and size for a 0.5 m object. Overlap: Shows what a single gold validation case looks like at metre scale; the method for future impactors. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14595)
- **NEOMOD3 (Nesvorny et al. 2024)**: Debiased NEO orbit, size and albedo model with a simulator; albedo model constrained by 428 cryogenic NEOWISE detections and extrapolated below 100 m. Overlap: Supplies the synthetic population for end-to-end injection tests and the source probabilities for channel 2. Its albedo model is weakest exactly at NHATS sizes. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18805)
- **RAVEN (2025) and ExoMiner (2022)**: Probabilistic exoplanet validation trained on synthetic injections and tested on external labelled sets; report precision at fixed thresholds. Overlap: Methodological model: synthetic training to reach parameter space the labels do not cover, then an external labelled exam. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17645)
- **Denario project (AI agents), random forest from orbital elements to spectral type**: Reported about 53% accuracy predicting spectral type from orbit alone. Overlap: An AI-agent precedent and a baseline number for orbit-only classification; population and validation design not checked. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26887)
- **Schmidt et al. 2020 (LSST DESC photo-z challenge) and Lima et al. 2008**: Evaluated probabilistic estimators trained on bright, biased labelled sets; showed ensemble calibration metrics can be gamed; reweighting of the labelled set to the target distribution. Overlap: The closest statistical analogue in astronomy to validating a probabilistic catalog with biased labels. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.03621)

### Opportunities

- **Publish frozen, timestamped predictions for objects about to receive ground truth, and keep a public scoreboard: Kamo'oalewa class and bulk density (sample November 2027), Dimorphos density (Hera 2027), Torifune class and hydration (flyby data pending), Apophis density (2029), 1998 KY26 class and density (2031), every new imminent impactor, and every NHATS object ahead of new spectra.** New: Only Chesley 2014 on Bennu did this, for one object. Kamo'oalewa already has a 7-sigma A2 and a new 15 to 21 m size, and no published density. Nobody runs a standing scoreboard. Feasibility: High. Needs only the catalog, a dated public commit and a hash. Fits the website naturally. Risk: Few scoring events per year, so it demonstrates honesty more than it measures calibration; a single miss on a high-profile target is very visible.
- **Build and publish a curated NEA truth table with tiers (sample, spacecraft mass, binary density, remote-sensing label, model-dependent) and per-channel independence flags.** New: No such table exists. SBDB is stale (no taxonomy for 1986 DA or 2016 ED85; Didymos density outdated) and SsODNet mixes Yarkovsky-derived densities with direct ones. Feasibility: High. About 40 gold objects and a few hundred silver rows; mostly careful literature work that agents can do and a fresh agent can audit. Risk: Judgement calls on conflicting literature values; needs primary papers, several behind paywalls.
- **Degrade-a-known-object tests as the catalog analogue of injection-recovery: cut the astrometry of Bennu, Itokawa, Ryugu, Apophis, Golevka, Moshup and 2000 DP107 down to NHATS-like arcs and observation counts, refit, and check that posteriors widen and still cover the full-data answer.** New: Yarkovsky papers report detections on the best data; none publishes coverage statistics as a function of arc length and observation count using real systematics. Feasibility: Medium. Needs a working orbit-fit pipeline with non-gravitational terms and MPC astrometry; compute is modest. Risk: Astrometric weighting and debiasing choices dominate results; reproducing JPL's A2 values first is a prerequisite and may be hard.
- **Mine the radar-detected NHATS subset (at least 226 objects) for published SC/OC ratios and radar albedos, which separate E and V types and flag metal candidates.** New: Radar is the largest physical-data overlap with NHATS after rotation periods, and the public summary tables have gone offline; nobody has assembled radar properties for the accessible list. Feasibility: Medium. Sources are PDS Asteroid Radar V18.0, Benner 2008, Virkki 2022 and single-object papers and abstracts. Risk: A detection is not a published polarisation ratio; the usable count may be a few dozen. High SC/OC is suggestive, not diagnostic.
- **A small-size bridge test set for the orbit-to-class prior: 13 pre-impact objects, 61 to 75 meteorite falls with orbits, MANOS (210), Lopez-Oquendo 2026 (122), scored with an explicit atmospheric-survival bias model.** New: The orbit-to-source prior has been built and checked on 100 m to 3 km objects. Testing it on metre-to-100 m objects with known material has not been done as a scored, pre-registered exercise. Feasibility: High for assembling; Meier's table is machine-readable and MANOS classes are in a paper table. Risk: Falls are filtered by the atmosphere (low-perihelion survivors over-represented, carbonaceous destroyed), so a naive score will mislead.
- **A size-truth audit for the H-to-diameter prior at metre to tens-of-metre scale, using objects with albedo-independent sizes: 1998 KY26, Kamo'oalewa, the impactors with energy-based sizes, 2011 MD and 2009 BD (Spitzer), radar-sized small NEAs.** New: Both small mission targets checked so far were 2 to 3 times smaller than assumed. Resource mass scales with the cube of size, so this prior matters more than class. Feasibility: High. Fewer than 30 objects; all in the literature. Risk: Discovery selection favours bright, high-albedo small objects, so the audit sample is biased in the same direction as the effect.

### Reasons it fails

- The gold set is too small to calibrate anything: about 12 objects with known composition and about 18 direct densities. It can catch gross over-confidence (77% power for 60% vs a claimed 90% at N = 12) and nothing subtler.
- The labelled sets sit 7 to 8 magnitudes brighter than NHATS (median H 17.9 to 19.1 against 26.2), and composition fractions change with size (MANOS, Lopez-Oquendo 2026). A good score on labelled objects does not transfer without a size-stratified test, and at H >= 22 only a few hundred labelled objects exist.
- The Yarkovsky density channel can be checked against independent truth on only 8 or 9 objects, all larger than 300 m. It already gives implausible answers for a tumbler (Toutatis 0.73 g/cm3) and two different answers for 1950 DA.
- Catalog values leak the answer: SsODNet densities for NHATS objects are mostly from Yarkovsky or radiation pressure, most SsODNet albedos there are derived values, diameters are often H plus an assumed albedo, and Marsset's per-source class fractions were fitted on the same MITHNEOS objects one would test on.
- Taxonomy is not composition. The X-complex is three materials separable only by albedo or radar; S vs Q is weathering; a C-type label does not say hydrated. A four-class 'metal / stony / carbonaceous-hydrated / other' truth label is itself an inference for nearly every silver object.
- The metal-rich class has one radar-confirmed NEA (1986 DA). A metal probability cannot be calibrated on NEAs; high radar albedo is not sufficient (2017 YE5).
- The volatile channel has no confirmed positive. None of the 14 dark comets has a detected coma or gas, the flagship 1998 KY26 signal fell from 4.2 to 1.5 sigma with more data, and ground-based 3-micron detections on NEAs are on S-types. It can ship only as an anomaly flag.
- SRP gives area-to-mass, not density; no SRP object has an independent density, and tiny objects with large accelerations can be artificial (2020 SO; the Phobos 1 claim for 1998 KY26).
- H-derived sizes for tens-of-metre objects were wrong by 2 to 3 times in both cases with truth (1998 KY26, Kamo'oalewa). Resource mass estimates inherit a factor of 10 to 30.
- Meteorite and fireball truth is filtered by the atmosphere and by recovery: low-perihelion survivors are up to 10 times over-represented and carbonaceous material is under-represented, so they cannot be used as an unbiased composition sample.
- Per-object source-region assignment is physically limited: the group that built the NEO model states that an individual NEO cannot be traced to a source and that per-object estimates over-weight dominant sources.
- Data access is fragile or restricted: FRIPON is non-commercial, DFN orbits are not public in bulk, Benner's radar tables are offline, CDS and the SsODNet bulk file were unreachable or throttled today, and MANOS spectra were not released.

## orbit-tooling

**Headline.** Refitting with non-gravitational parameters works today on this workstation (GRSS, cross-checked with layup, matched JPL's A1/A2 within 1 sigma on 8 of 9 test objects in seconds per fit), but 83-89% of NHATS objects have arcs too short to carry any non-grav information and JPL/ESA have already extracted the strong signals. The defensible contribution is a uniform open catalog of A1/A2 posteriors including non-detections, with injection-recovery efficiencies, concentrated on about 890 NHATS objects; for the most accessible targets the useful channel is radiation pressure (area-to-mass), not Yarkovsky.

HANDS-ON RESULT (all run 2026-11-20). `uv pip install grss` installed GRSS 4.6.0 (released 2026-10-26, GPL-3.0, C++/Python, authors Makadia/Farnocchia/Chesley/Eggl) in 5.6 s; first import downloaded 4.3 GB of SPICE kernels plus the Eggl 2020 debias tables in 2 min 27 s (peak 7.3 GB RAM). Using MPC `get-obs` ADES astrometry, JPL radar, Eggl debiasing, Veres 2017 weights, and non-grav parameters started at zero, GRSS gave: (480883) 2001 YE4 A2 = -6.909e-14 +- 7.4e-16 au/d^2 vs JPL -6.950e-14 +- 9.2e-16 (0.35 sigma; ESA NEOCC file says -6.919e-14, S/N 95); 2011 PT -2.126e-13 +- 3.0e-14 vs JPL -2.121e-13 +- 3.0e-14; 2009 BD A1 5.28e-11 +- 9.7e-12 vs 5.60e-11 +- 8.5e-12 and A2 -1.12e-12 vs -1.15e-12; 2012 LA A1 6.50e-11 vs 6.71e-11; 2003 RM A2 3.296e-12 vs 3.287e-12; Nyx and Aten (with Gaia FPR) within 1 sigma; NHATS object 2026 QQ8 A1 1.35e-10 +- 9.6e-12 vs 1.31e-10 +- 1.0e-11. Fit time on one core: 0.1-0.5 s for typical NHATS arcs, 1.5-5 s for multi-apparition objects, 13-22 s for 24-64 year arcs with radar. A second engine, layup 1.1.0 (MIT, on ASSIST/REBOUND, own IOD), reproduced 2001 YE4 from the raw MPC file with no JPL input (-6.938e-14 +- 1.6e-15) and three others with a JPL warm start. layup needed a uv-managed Python to build (system Python lacks headers).

FAILURES. 2020 CD3 (minimoon, NHATS delta-v 3.57 km/s) failed in both engines. GRSS hit its iteration cap on 44 of 192 pilot fits, all for arcs of 24 days or less. layup's IOD failed on 4 of 6 Earth-like-orbit objects until warm-started. Bennu came out 2-3% from published values (-19.45e-4 vs Chesley 2014 -19.0 +- 0.1 e-4 au/My) against a 0.5% formal error: formal sigmas understate inter-pipeline differences. Gravity-only fits absorbed the signal by rejecting observations (72 of 450 for 2001 YE4; 490 of 603 for Bennu), so chi-square comparisons need a frozen outlier mask. Reduced chi-square was 0.15-0.5 (conservative weights), so chi-square thresholds are not calibrated probabilities. Six parallel requests broke JPL's "one API request at a time" rule and 4 of 64 SBDB calls failed.

SCALE (JPL SBDB + NHATS, today). NHATS has 7,226 objects: median arc 12 days; arc <=30 d 71.3%, <=90 d 81.7%, <=365 d 85.4%; median 37 observations. In a 64-object stratified pilot no object with arc under 90 days came within 77x of radiation-pressure sensitivity and none under ~3 years could see Yarkovsky. Informative pools: 814 NHATS objects with arc >1,000 d (651 without a JPL A2) and 74 with H>=27 and arc >=60 d (63 without a JPL A1). Among the 286 with delta-v <5 km/s, 38 (13%) are in the radiation-pressure pool and 16 already have a JPL A1 or A2. Across all 43,151 NEAs, 10,487 have arcs >1,000 d. JPL already lists A2 for 618 NEAs (573 at S/N>=3; arcs: 5th percentile 8 yr, median 22 yr), A1 for 21, A3 for 9; 169 NHATS objects have a JPL A2 and 10 an A1 at S/N>=3. ### Prior art

- **ESA NEOCC automated Yarkovsky pipeline (Fenucci et al. 2024, A&A; arXiv 2311.10175)**: Systematic, automated A2 search over all 32,396 known NEAs with S/N>=3, a Monte Carlo physical-compatibility test and an isolated-tracklet test; 348 accepted detections; rerun after every monthly MPC update and served through the NEOCC portal. Also fits area-to-mass for small objects (new SRP detection on 2021 GM1). Overlap: Almost complete overlap with channel (1) for Yarkovsky. It publishes accepted detections only, uses closed software (Aegis), and reports no injection-recovery efficiency or upper limits. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10175)
- **JPL Small-Body Database non-gravitational solutions**: Operational orbit solutions with A2 for 618 NEAs (573 at S/N>=3), A1 for 21 and A3 for 9 as of 2026-11-20, with sigmas and covariances via API. Overlap: 169 NHATS objects already have a JPL A2 and 10 a significant A1. It is both the validation truth set and the reason new detections will be scarce. (https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/sbdb.html)
- **Greenberg et al. 2020, Yarkovsky Drift Detections for 247 Near-Earth Asteroids**: 600 candidates fitted with IDOS/MONTE; 247 detections via F-test (p<=0.05) and robustness tests; population tests of 1/D scaling and retrograde excess; median Yarkovsky efficiency 12%. Overlap: Largest published detection table and the source of the drop-earliest-observations and pre-1965 tests; software is not open. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05513)
- **Del Vigna et al. 2018, Detecting the Yarkovsky effect among near-Earth asteroids from astrometric data**: OrbFit fits of 538 NEAs: 87 accepted, 24 marginal, 14 rejected with documented reasons; 4 radiation-pressure detections; comparison with JPL. Overlap: Defines the S/N>=3 and S<=2 acceptance scheme and supplies a ready list of negative controls. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05947)
- **Farnocchia et al. 2013, Near Earth Asteroids with measurable Yarkovsky effect**: First systematic all-NEA search; 21 reliable detections; quantified that 67% of S/N>3 results are spurious without a physical filter; documented precovery and pre-CCD traps. Overlap: Sets the false-positive prior the project must beat and the Bennu-scaled expected-value test. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.4812)
- **Dziadura et al. 2023 and 2024 (Gaia DR3 and FPR)**: Refitted 446 NEAs with Gaia astrometry using OrbFit; 49 (DR3) and 43 (FPR) Yarkovsky detections; the 2023 paper converts A2 to bulk density. Overlap: Direct prior art for 'Yarkovsky -> density' and for ingesting Gaia SSO astrometry; the same group will likely refit with DR4. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09750)
- **Seligman et al. 2023 and follow-ups (dark comets); Farnocchia et al. 2023 on 2003 RM**: Case-by-case detection of non-radial accelerations (A2, A3) far above Yarkovsky on inactive-looking NEOs; 14 objects by the 2024 PNAS paper; 1998 SH2 added in 2026. Overlap: Covers the 'dark comet as volatile indicator' part of channel (1). Detections came from targeted follow-up and remeasurement, not blind fitting; all need multi-apparition arcs. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08115)
- **GRSS (Makadia, Farnocchia, Chesley, Eggl 2025, PSJ 6, 85; JOSS 10, 7861)**: Open-source propagator and orbit-determination library with JPL-style debiasing, weighting, radar, Gaia and non-grav estimation; validated against JPL operational tools. Overlap: The enabling tool: it makes a JPL-grade refit scriptable in Python on one workstation. (https://github.com/rahil-makadia/grss)
- **layup (Holman et al., CfA/LINCC, for Rubin)**: LSST-scale orbit fitting on ASSIST/REBOUND with IOD, non-grav fitting including an automatic model ladder, radar, debias and weights. Overlap: Independent second engine; its 'auto' ladder and thresholds are a ready-made (uncalibrated) model-selection scheme. (https://github.com/Smithsonian/layup)
- **Micheli et al. 2012-2014, Mommert et al. 2014, Farnocchia et al. 2017 (radiation pressure on 2009 BD, 2012 LA, 2011 MD, 2015 TC25)**: Measured area-to-mass ratios of meter-scale NEOs from astrometry and, with a size, inferred bulk density. Overlap: Prior art for the SRP channel on exactly the most accessible NHATS objects; done object by object, not as a catalog. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.0564)

### Opportunities

- **Publish a uniform A1/A2 posterior for every object with enough arc, including upper limits and explicit 'no information' entries, rather than detections only. Stage 1: about 890 NHATS objects (814 with arc >1,000 d, 74 with H>=27 and arc >=60 d). Stage 2: about 10,500 multi-apparition NEAs.** New: JPL lists A2 only where it was adopted in the solution and NEOCC lists accepted detections only; I found no public catalog of non-detection constraints. Upper limits are what a probabilistic density prior needs for the other ~700 NHATS multi-apparition objects. Feasibility: High. GRSS runs at 0.1-5 s per fit; all NHATS is 10-20 minutes on 32 cores, all NEAs about 4 hours. Data pull is 2-24 hours of polite sequential API calls. Risk: An upper limit on A2 does not bound density without an obliquity prior, so most entries will be weakly informative; formal sigmas need empirical calibration before they can be called calibrated.
- **Measure detection efficiency by injection-recovery: inject synthetic A1/A2 into each object's real observation times and error model, refit, and publish per-object 50% and 90% recovery amplitudes plus an empirical false-alarm rate from parameters with no expected signal.** New: None of the published Yarkovsky pipelines (Farnocchia 2013, Del Vigna 2018, Greenberg 2020, Fenucci 2024) reports a recovery efficiency; they rely on a physical-plausibility filter instead. Feasibility: High for the 888 informative NHATS objects (about 50 core-hours); about 600 core-hours for all multi-apparition NEAs. Risk: Injected noise that is Gaussian and uncorrelated will overstate efficiency; real errors are systematic (timing, catalog, isolated tracklets). The noise model must be bootstrapped from real residuals by station and epoch.
- **Make radiation pressure (area-to-mass) the headline non-grav channel for the most accessible targets: 38 of the 286 NHATS objects with delta-v < 5 km/s have H>=27 and arc >=60 d, and 10 already have a JPL A1.** New: Published SRP work is object by object; no systematic A/m catalog with upper limits exists for the mission-accessible list, and A/m plus a size prior gives density directly without the obliquity degeneracy of Yarkovsky. Feasibility: Medium. Fits are sub-second. Recovered today for 2009 BD, 2012 LA and 2026 QQ8. Risk: The best targets are the hardest numerically: 2020 CD3 failed in both engines and 2006 RH120 shows non-SRP accelerations. Density from A/m inherits the albedo uncertainty in size (factor ~2 in diameter).
- **Two-engine reproduction of the JPL and NEOCC detection lists (573 JPL A2 at S/N>=3) as an open, rerunnable artifact, with per-object differences against JPL sigmas and NEOCC .rwo weights and rejections.** New: The three operational centres use closed or hard-to-script software; an open reproduction with GRSS and layup and a published diff does not appear to exist. Feasibility: High; this is the natural 'recover known objects first' stage and a few hours of compute. Risk: It is replication, not discovery. Agreement is partly built in when fits start from JPL elements with the same weights and debias tables; layup IOD or find_orb is needed for true independence.
- **Per-object 'what would it take' forecasts: for each NHATS object, the observation windows and astrometric accuracy that would bring A1 or A2 to S/N 3 (GRSS accepts simulated observations).** New: Turns the 83-89% 'no information' majority into an actionable observing list and a dynamic website feature; not offered by JPL or NEOCC. Feasibility: Medium. Needs ephemeris and observability computation on top of covariance analysis; cheap computationally. Risk: Many NHATS objects have such poor orbits (condition code 6-9 for 5,045 of 7,226) that recovery itself is uncertain, which dominates any forecast.
- **Refit with Gaia DR4 epoch astrometry as soon as it is released on 2 December 2026.** New: Timely; Gaia transits sharply raise A2 S/N (Nyx went from JPL S/N 4.7 to 6.9 in our fit with 75 FPR observations). Feasibility: Medium. GRSS already ingests gaiadr3 and gaiafpr tables; the DR4 table name and error model must be added. Risk: Gaia sees only bright NEAs (446 in FPR), almost none of them NHATS targets, and JPL, NEOCC and the Poznan group will do the same refit quickly.

### Reasons it fails

- For 83% of NHATS objects (arc of 90 days or less) the astrometry carries no non-gravitational information at all, and for Yarkovsky the figure is about 89%; pilot sensitivities were 2 to 5 orders of magnitude short. A catalog that reports non-grav constraints 'per object' will be empty for most of the list.
- JPL (618 NEAs with A2, 21 with A1) and ESA NEOCC (348 accepted, monthly reruns) have already harvested the significant signals. New detections from a fresh pipeline will sit at S/N 3 to 5, where Farnocchia et al. 2013 found 67 to 88% of unfiltered detections spurious.
- A2 constrains density only through a product with obliquity, thermal inertia, rotation and size; a detection gives an upper bound, a non-detection gives nothing without an obliquity prior. A1 gives area-to-mass and needs a size. Presenting these as density measurements would mislead.
- Formal uncertainties are not calibrated: reduced chi-square of 0.15-0.5 from conservative weights, yet a 2-3% offset from published Bennu values against a 0.5% formal error. 'Calibrated uncertainty' needs empirical work the literature has not done.
- Model comparison is easy to get wrong: outlier rejection under the gravity-only model removed 72 of 450 (2001 YE4) and 490 of 603 (Bennu) observations, making chi-square differences meaningless unless the mask is frozen.
- The decisive false-positive checks cannot be automated by agents: remeasuring old plates (1992 BF, Kamo'oalewa), judging isolated tracklets, obtaining occultation or spacecraft astrometry that never reached the MPC (Didymos, Phaethon).
- Tool fragility on exactly the interesting objects: both engines failed on the minimoon 2020 CD3; GRSS failed to converge on 23% of pilot fits (all short arcs); layup's IOD failed on 4 of 6 Earth-like orbits; the primary engine is a GPL-3 package with one main maintainer.
- Independence is weaker than it looks: starting from JPL elements with JPL's debias tables and JPL-derived weights reproduces JPL partly by construction.
- Service constraints: JPL requires one request at a time and a contact-bearing User-Agent; MPC documents no API policy and its bulk files are five months stale; the MPC database replica needs a dedicated always-on server.

### Open questions

- Will the project send JPL a User-Agent with product name, version and contact details, as the API terms require for automated clients? This needs the owner's decision.
- Is GPL-3.0 (GRSS, ASSIST, REBOUND) acceptable for the published pipeline and website code?
- Will the MPC accept about 43,000 sequential get-obs calls, or should the project use the paid BigQuery replica or ask the helpdesk for a bulk pull?
- Should the catalog publish upper limits and 'no information' entries for every object? I recommend yes.
- Is there really no published catalog of A1/A2 upper limits or any injection-recovery efficiency study? My search was time-boxed; the literature researcher should confirm.
- What does Gaia DR4 add for near-Earth asteroids beyond the 446 in the FPR, and what are the DR4 SSO table names and error model?
- Can GRSS's short-arc and captured-object failures be fixed by fitting Cartesian states at a mid-arc epoch, or must those objects go to find_orb? Not resolved in the time box.
- Where is the current NEOCC Yarkovsky list in bulk form? The portal reports 'more than 340' detections updated monthly, but the computer-access page lists no dedicated file.
- Which pre-registered thresholds should be adopted: S/N>=3 plus NEOCC's 95th-percentile physical test, or a stricter S/N>=5 for anything not already in JPL or NEOCC lists?

## presentation

**Headline.** Ship a fully static, table-first site (generator binary, plain JS, one 1-3 MB data file) whose distinguishing features are per-object evidence ledgers, a public known-answers test and visible version diffs; skip the 3D orrery. Credibility depends on a human arXiv endorser and reviewer, time-stamped prospective predictions and an RNAAS note with a Zenodo DOI, not on the site.

LANDSCAPE. No existing site shows a per-object probability, its evidence trail, or how often it is right. Orreries (NASA Eyes on Asteroids: 42,580 NEOs, 3.56 MB of JS, no composition; Rubin Orbitviewer: 16,000 to 1,000,000 objects, outreach only; JPL and ESA NEOCC viewers) are decorative at population scale. Catalog sites (SsODNet, MP3C with 1,461,616 bodies and 6,113,272 measured properties, JPL NHATS with 7,226 objects) show measured values only; NHATS already publishes "Next Optical Opportunity", so that idea alone is not new. The two "mining target" sites are dead or stale: Asterank's API still prices Ryugu at $82.76 billion with no uncertainty, its Ryugu record ends at 2016-08-09 and its repo was last pushed 2023-05-01; ECOCEL (Kovalenko et al. 2022) now serves an empty 3.2 KB shell. Useful ideas to borrow: NASA Eyes' "Watch" jump-to-event buttons, NEOCC's synodic-frame view, SsODNet's per-value citations and per-object JSON (ssoCard), meteoriteorbits.info's dated "as of" line (61 meteorites, 22 Sept 2026), NEOfixer's published score formula. Best models for explaining the method: Ciechanowski's "Moon" (two hand-written script files, 34 KB + 551 KB, raw WebGL, no libraries), Distill's Gaussian-process article (click evidence, posterior updates; DOI, BibTeX, public reviews), The Pudding's Birthday Paradox (guess, then reveal). ### Opportunities

- **Evidence ledger per object: a log-odds waterfall from orbit prior through each channel to the posterior, every step linked to its measurement and citation, with channels that can be switched off.** New: No reviewed site shows how a conclusion about an asteroid was reached. SsODNet and MP3C cite values; none shows evidence combining. Feasibility: High if the inference factorises by channel (about 24 numbers per object, recomputed in the browser). Otherwise the pipeline must precompute leave-one-out posteriors. Risk: For most objects only the orbit prior contributes, so most ledgers are one bar; that must be shown honestly.
- **Known-answers page: guess-then-reveal cards for Bennu, Ryugu, Itokawa, Eros, Didymos, Kamoʻoalewa, 2008 TC3, 2024 BX1 and meteorite falls with orbits, plus a reliability diagram over all objects with measured classes.** New: No asteroid site publishes its own calibration; the best general example (FiveThirtyEight's) is offline. Feasibility: High. Needs withheld-truth predictions and a cited truth table; static SVG plus a few lines of JS. Risk: The famous objects are large and well observed, unlike the small targets; the test may flatter the method unless small-object cases (impactors, meteorite falls) are included.
- **Prospector's plane: round-trip delta-v against probability of water or metal, each object drawn as a credible-interval bar, brushing linked to the table, Pareto front marked.** New: Asterank and ECOCEL showed point values in tables; NHATS has no composition axis. Feasibility: High. Canvas 2D over 7,226 objects; NHATS fields plus class posteriors. Risk: If posteriors sit near the prior the plot is a uniform smear; needs a 'moved by evidence' encoding so the informative few stand out.
- **'What would one observation buy?' list: objects ranked by expected information gain from one colour, albedo or spectrum, filtered by NHATS observing window, magnitude and hemisphere.** New: NHATS lists observing opportunities and NEOfixer ranks for orbit improvement; nobody ranks accessible NEAs by compositional information gain. Feasibility: Medium. Needs simulated measurement likelihoods per channel; the observing windows are already in the NHATS API. Risk: Observers will act on it only if the community trusts the method; wrong priorities waste scarce telescope time.
- **Catalog as a diff: version date and 'what changed' on the home page, per-object posterior history across releases, immutable per-version files with a moving latest pointer.** New: Rubin's discovery counter is the only liveness signal among reviewed sites; none shows how conclusions change. Feasibility: High. A diff table per release from content-addressed snapshots; weekly deterministic rebuild. Risk: Silent breakage when upstream formats change; needs a validation gate before deploy.
- **Null results as first-class pages: per channel, how many objects it informed, where it has no power, with injection-recovery sensitivity curves and a site-wide toggle to grey out objects no better than prior.** New: Matches the claudestronomy standard and RNAAS scope; no catalog site shows where it knows nothing. Feasibility: High. Per-object information gain and recovery curves are pipeline by-products. Risk: May reveal that the catalog adds little for most objects, which is a real result but a weaker headline.
- **Set-your-own-assumptions ranking: sliders for resource yield per class and prior weights, live re-sort, and a stability indicator for the top 50.** New: Replaces Asterank's hidden fixed assumptions with visible, adjustable ones. Feasibility: High. Pure client-side arithmetic on class posteriors. Risk: Visitors can dial in a flattering answer and quote it without context.
- **Synodic accessibility view for a selected object and its neighbours in the Earth-rotating frame, with return times and NHATS launch windows, drawn by one small WebGL2 program.** New: Uses the frame that matters for access instead of the heliocentric hairball every orrery shows; NEOCC has it only as a single-object expert tool. Feasibility: Medium. Two-body propagation is enough for display; about 300 lines. Risk: Two-body paths mislead near close approaches; needs JPL's own disclaimer and links to precise tools.
- **Machine twin of everything: versioned Parquet and CSV, schema.json, latest.json manifest with hashes and DOI, per-object JSON, open CORS, a short llms.txt.** New: JPL forbids embedding its APIs and SsODNet has no simple static bulk-plus-per-object pattern with versioned inference results. Feasibility: High. Falls out of the static build at no extra runtime cost. Risk: Per-object files for all 43,151 objects exceed the 20,000-file free hosting limit unless sharded.
- **Explorable paper: a Ciechanowski/Distill-style article teaching each channel with one manipulable figure on real data, carrying DOI, citation block and public reviews.** New: No asteroid-characterisation method has been explained this way; it turns 'how we know' into the product. Feasibility: Medium to low. Four to six bespoke figures of 200 to 500 lines each; only sensible after the method is stable. Risk: High craft cost for a solo owner; few readers interact, so the static text and images must carry the argument alone.

### Reasons it fails

- The site can only be as interesting as the posterior. If most of the 7,226 accessible objects end near their orbit prior, the trade-off plane is a smear and the evidence ledgers are single bars; a polished interface over an uninformative catalog would mislead by its polish.
- False precision is the default failure of this genre: Asterank still shows $82.76 billion for Ryugu. Any single ranked number, dollar figure or 'top 10 water asteroids' list will be quoted without its interval.
- The known-answers test can flatter the method: Bennu, Ryugu, Itokawa and Eros are large and richly observed, while the targets are metre-scale with only an orbit. Without small-object truth cases the calibration claim does not transfer.
- arXiv is gated: since 21 January 2026 an unaffiliated first-time author needs a personal endorsement, and unchecked LLM output now risks a one-year ban. Without a human endorser and reviewer from the NEO community the work stays a website.
- The reception climate is wary of AI-run science. Professionals' scarce resource is follow-up and review time; a catalog that asks for attention without prospective, time-stamped predictions will be ignored.
- Licences can block the obvious design: Gaia data are CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO with authorisation needed for commercial use, LCDB is all rights reserved, the MPC replica is licensed for internal use. A CC BY catalog framed as commercial prospecting that republishes upstream values would be in breach.
- JPL forbids embedding its APIs in websites and allows one request at a time, and warns formats can change without notice. A thin live client will not work, and a scheduled rebuild can break silently.
- Hosted interactive sites die: ECOCEL is an empty shell, Asterank is frozen, R2D3 and FiveThirtyEight's calibration page are gone. Any server, framework or build chain adds a way for a solo-maintained site to rot.
- A 3D orrery would absorb the build budget and duplicate what NASA and Rubin do with far more resources, while conveying nothing about composition.
- Most readers do not interact with interactive content. If the key findings need a click, brush or slider to appear, most visitors will never see them.
- Per-object static files for all 43,151 NEAs (86,302 files) exceed the 20,000-file limit on Cloudflare's free tier; a naive build fails at deploy.
- Client-side 'toggle a channel' recomputation assumes a factorised model. If the science plan uses a joint model, the interaction needs precomputed leave-one-out results or it shows numbers the pipeline never produced.
- Claudestronomy is framed around finding new objects and macrogrants cannot fund for-profit initiatives; a characterisation catalog with a resource-prospecting framing may not fit without asking.

## novelty

**Headline.** Nobody has published a per-object composition posterior joined to NHATS accessibility, but every ingredient already exists (Jedicke 2018, MANOS, METEOMOD, classy, Asterank), so the only defensible novelty is a scored calibration audit plus a time-stamped forward forecast, not a new method. The brief's "none of the 286 has a taxonomy in SBDB" is true but an SBDB artifact (SBDB types only 327 of 43,360 NEOs) and must be redone against the literature.

SCOPE. Application-led sweep (web search, ten arXiv API queries 2011 to Nov 2026, EPSC/DPS 2025-2026 abstracts, company and agency pages). Full report with tables and about 100 URLs is at the report path.

BASELINE RE-CHECKED TODAY (JPL APIs). NHATS lists 7,226 objects; 286 have minimum round-trip delta-v under 5 km/s (40 under 4; 726 under 6). Median H is 26.2. Only 23 of 7,226 have a spectral type in SBDB and 0 of the 286 do. But SBDB holds a type for just 327 of 43,360 NEOs, while MITHNEOS has spectra of more than 1,000 NEOs and Sergeyev et al. 2023 classify 7,401 planet-crossers. "Uncharacterized" must be re-established against SsODNet, MANOS, UKIRT and Sergeyev before it is claimed.

ALREADY DONE.
(1) Probabilistic taxonomy from partial data: Mahlke et al. 2022 "classy" (2,125 asteroids, 17 classes, uses albedo); Mommert 2016 and Erasmus 2017 for tiny NEOs; Dotson & Mathias 2017 (NASA Ames) Bayesian class-from-albedo, later a Bayesian network for class, porosity and density used in Planetary Defense Conference exercises.
(2) Orbit to source to family to meteorite: Granvik 2018 and NEOMOD give per-object source probabilities; Brož et al. 2024 (38 families) and the public METEOMOD tool return per-orbit probabilities for about 50 families grouped by meteorite class.
(3) Orbit and size to taxonomy to water for low-delta-v NEOs: Jedicke et al. 2018 did the whole chain at population level ("likely thousands of H2O-rich NEOs larger than about 5 m" with return delta-v under about 3 km/s). Deshapriya et al. 2023 asked directly whether class can be guessed from orbit at discovery.
(4) Population counts: Elvis 2014 (about 10 PGM-ore NEOs; about 9,000 water-ore NEOs down to 18 m); Rivkin & DeMeo 2019 (700 +/- 350 hydrated accessible NEOs; no per-object list); USGS ASTRA 2017 (428 objects, Monte Carlo, unclassified objects drawn from a 27% C / 34% M / 39% S prior; "not a usable assessment").
(5) Measured answer for the target population: MANOS spectra (210 NEOs, mean H about 25, mean delta-v about 5.7 km/s; S+Q 43.8%, X 23.8%) and MANOS colors (189 NEOs; S fraction about 65% at km scale falling to about one third below 50 m). UKIRT (122 NEOs, 5 to 150 m) finds the debiased small population dominated by C/X; Sanchez 2024 (84 spectra under 300 m) finds 66% S. The known answers disagree.
(6) Non-gravitational: Yarkovsky densities for 49 NEAs (Dziadura 2023), thermal inertia for 38 (ASTERIA), radiation-pressure area-to-mass for a handful of H>27 objects, six "dark comets" (Seligman 2023). Hansen & Hirabayashi (6 Nov 2026) argue 1998 KY26 cannot retain volatiles, so acceleration alone does not show water.
(7) Metal flags: Harris & Drube 2014 list 18 NEOs (eta > 2.0, IR albedo 0.15 to 0.30), all 0.36 to 3 km; 7 of the 10 typed ones are S or Q.
(8) Interactive sites: Asterank (2012, open source, acquired by Planetary Resources 2013; deterministic value from spectral type and delta-v) and ECOCEL (2022; 326 typed asteroids; reported offline).

WHY PRIORS ARE WEAK. Morbidelli 2020: low and high albedo NEOs are "well mixed in orbital space". Sanchez 2024: 83% of small NEOs come via nu6. López-Oquendo 2026 reportedly got a maximum METEOMOD source probability above 50% for only about 20% of objects. For Earth-like NHATS orbits the source posterior is nearly identical across objects. McGraw 2026 finds 3-micron hydration on S and V types, tied to low inclination and large aphelion, so "hydrated = carbonaceous" fails. The lowest-delta-v entries include minimoons (2026 QQ8, 2020 CD3) and reported lunar ejecta (2024 PT5); NEO source models have no lunar source (Alessi & Jedicke 2026: about 70 or more lunar co-orbitals above 10 m versus about 1,600 main-belt).

COMPETITORS. Dorsey & Granvik (EPSC2026-1111) are building class-specific NEO orbit models; Granvik also co-authors Karman+ abstracts (targets 2022 DC5, 2021 UH2; chosen by accessibility, H, spin). AstroForge names 2022 OB5 and uses Lowell telescope time, no published method. Rubin is expected to give griz for about 4,000 NEOs with D >= 10 m and Euclid NIR classes for about 10^4.

AI AGENTS. claudestronomy.com is run by macrogrants (incorporated 10 Oct 2026, director Will DePue); its rules match the brief; no minor-planet result is listed on any of the four sites. The Depue, Wolk, Farritor dwarf-planet candidates (X, 11 Nov 2026; "1/4 the width of Pluto", "twice as far") have no designation, archive name, MPC record, method or professional comment in anything I could read; only aggregator coverage. It is not Rubin's 2026 ED138 (Holman, MPEC 27 Oct 2026). Ilvarra, a self-described Claude agent, says it sent 24 unmatched DECam objects to the MPC. The MPC October 2026 newsletter says "Using an automated tool should never mean submitting results without understanding or verifying them" and promises stricter observatory-code rules. AAS Journals now require AI-use disclosure. No agent work on asteroid composition or resources was found.

VERDICT. Open, most defensible first: (1) scored held-out calibration of orbit, H and sparse-data class priors on small NEOs (log loss, reliability curves against the base rate), plus frozen NHATS forecasts scored as Rubin, Euclid, SPHEREx and NEO Surveyor labels arrive; (2) the joined public catalog with an information-value "observe next" ranking, which NHATS, NEOfixer, Asterank and ECOCEL do not provide; (3) lunar and artificial provenance classes for the lowest-delta-v objects; (4) non-gravitational signals as a filter, not a composition measurement; (5) agent-run pre-registered process, a process claim only.

REFEREE'S THREE: Jedicke et al. 2018 (PSS 159, 28); Devogèle et al. 2019 with Moskovitz et al. 2026 (MANOS); Brož et al. 2024 (A&A 689, A183) with METEOMOD. Runners-up: Deshapriya 2023, Dotson & Mathias 2017, Morbidelli 2020 / NEOMOD3, Keszthelyi 2017, Mahlke 2022, Sergeyev 2023.

### Prior art

- **Jedicke, Sercel, Gillis-Davis, Morenz & Gertsch 2018, PSS 159, 28**: Built NEO taxonomy as a function of orbit and size from an NEO model plus main-belt source taxonomy, assigned classes to a synthetic low-delta-v population, applied meteorite-analog water contents, counted water-bearing NEOs versus return delta-v. Checked only against the C:S ratio of small NEOs. No per-object catalog. Overlap: Very high: the orbit-to-source-to-class-to-water chain, at population level. Leaves per-object posteriors for real NHATS objects and calibration open. (https://scholarsmine.mst.edu/geosci_geo_peteng_facwork/1362)
- **MANOS: Devogèle et al. 2019 (AJ 158, 196) and Moskovitz et al. 2026 (PSJ, arXiv 2603.15872)**: Visible spectra of 210 and griz colors of 189 low-delta-v sub-km NEOs. Shows S fraction falling and X-complex rising toward small sizes. Survey ongoing since 2013. Overlap: This is the measured ground truth for the target population and the main validation set; it also shows priors from large NEOs do not transfer. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15872)
- **Brož et al. 2024 (A&A 689, A183) and METEOMOD web tool**: Family-by-family contributions to metre- and km-sized NEOs for 38 families; public tool returning per-orbit probabilities for about 50 source families grouped by meteorite class. Validated on meteorite falls with orbits. Code downloadable. Overlap: Channel 2 (orbit to family to meteorite type) already exists as public code; built for metre-size bodies. (https://sirrah.troja.mff.cuni.cz/~mira/meteomod/meteomod.php)
- **López-Oquendo, Trilling, Mommert & Marsset 2026 (accepted PSJ)**: Probabilistic ML classes from UKIRT ZJHK for 122 NEOs of 5 to 150 m, debiased by size; applied METEOMOD per object. Overlap: Closest existing 'probabilistic class plus source family for decametre NEOs' paper; reports weak per-object source discrimination. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28779)
- **Mahlke, Carry & Mattei 2022, A&A 665, A26 (classy)**: Probabilistic taxonomy from partial spectra and albedo; 2,125 asteroids, 17 classes; tool and class tables public. Overlap: Covers the sparse spectra/albedo fusion channel; reference implementation to use, not to rebuild. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11229)
- **Dotson & Mathias 2017; Dotson, Wheeler, Rumpf, Mathias 2019-2023 (NASA Ames)**: Bayesian class-from-albedo; then a Bayesian network over albedo, class, diameter, porosity, density, strength, used in PDC 2021/2023/2025 exercises. Conference abstracts and slides only. Overlap: Per-object probabilistic class and density from sparse data, built for planetary defence; same structure as the proposed catalog. (https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20170009812)
- **Deshapriya et al. 2023, A&A 674, A50**: G-mode clustering of NEO orbital elements to see whether class can be guessed at discovery; orbital regimes mapped to primitive or siliceous. Dataset at CDS. Overlap: Direct precedent for orbit-only class guesses; rule-based, no calibrated probabilities reported. (https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/abs/2023/06/aa45316-22/aa45316-22.html)
- **Granvik et al. 2018 NEO model / ESA NEOPOP; Morbidelli et al. 2020; Nesvorný et al. 2024 NEOMOD3**: Per-object source-region probabilities; debiased albedo distribution by orbit and size (two Rayleigh components, about 0.03 and 0.17); public population generators. Overlap: The standard source and albedo priors; authors state albedo is well mixed in orbit space and extrapolated below 100 m. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.03550)
- **Dorsey & Granvik, EPSC2026-1111**: Class-specific NEO orbit models to give probabilistic source assessments per taxonomic class; in progress. Overlap: Live competitor on the orbit-to-class prior from the NEO-model author, who also works with Karman+. (https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EPSC2026/EPSC2026-1111.html)
- **Marsset et al. 2022 (AJ 163, 165) and Binzel et al. 2019 (Icarus 324), MITHNEOS**: Spectra of more than 1,000 NEOs; debiased class fractions by escape route: S/Q fall and carbonaceous rise with source distance; D-type excess. Overlap: Establishes and quantifies the orbit-composition gradient; core training and test data. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13796)
- **Sergeyev, Carry et al. 2023, A&A 679, A148**: Fused SDSS, SkyMapper, Gaia and ground colors into taxonomy for 7,401 planet-crossing asteroids; catalog at CDS. Overlap: The multi-catalog fusion channel, done; only 42 mission-accessible objects. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03555)
- **Rivkin & DeMeo 2019, JGR Planets; Elvis 2014, PSS 91, 20; Elvis & Esty 2014**: Population estimates of hydrated and ore-bearing NEOs by size and accessibility; number of assay probes needed and the value of prior characterization. Overlap: Population-level resource framing and the value-of-information argument; no per-object output. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02285)
- **USGS ASTRA: Keszthelyi et al. 2017, OFR 2017-1041**: Tested USGS three-part mineral assessment on 428 km-scale low-delta-v NEOs with carbonaceous/metal-rich/stony deposit types and a Monte Carlo over assigned classes. No validation; declared not a usable assessment. Overlap: Same three-way class scheme and prior-assignment step; a resource-side referee will cite it. (https://www.usgs.gov/centers/gggsc/science/astra-asteroid-resource-assessment)
- **Harris & Drube 2014, ApJL 785, L4**: Used NEATM beaming parameter from WISE as a metal indicator; 18 candidate NEOs of 0.36 to 3 km; checked against radar albedo for 12 NEOs. Overlap: The metal-rich channel; does not reach decametre NHATS objects and most typed candidates are S or Q. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.6346)
- **Asterank (Webster 2012) and ECOCEL (Kovalenko et al. 2022, PSS)**: Interactive sites ranking asteroids by value and accessibility (Asterank, open source) or supporting mission planning with composition from spectral class (ECOCEL, 326 typed objects, reported offline). Overlap: The website precedent. Deterministic, no uncertainty, limited to typed objects. (https://www.asterank.com/about)
- **Dziadura et al. 2023 (A&A 680, A77); Novaković et al. 2024 (ASTERIA); Del Vigna et al. 2018**: Yarkovsky A2 and bulk densities for 49 NEAs from Gaia DR3; thermal inertia from Yarkovsky for 38 NEAs; 87 Yarkovsky detections and 4 radiation-pressure detections (H > 27 only). Overlap: Channel 1 already done on samples by the astrometry specialists; not framed as resource indicators and not catalog-wide for NHATS sizes. (https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/abs/2023/12/aa47342-23/aa47342-23.html)
- **Seligman et al. 2023, 2024; Taylor et al. 2024; Hansen & Hirabayashi 2026**: Defined dark comets (six, later more), split them into two populations, proposed dynamical origins; the 2026 paper argues 1998 KY26 cannot hold volatiles. Overlap: The dark-comet volatile indicator exists and is now directly contested. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10275)
- **McGraw, Thomas, Emery & Rivkin 2022, 2026**: 3-micron spectroscopy of 29 + 15 NEOs; hydration bands on S and V types, correlated with low inclination and large aphelion. Overlap: An orbit-based predictor of surface hydration already published; breaks the hydrated-equals-carbonaceous shortcut. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00430)
- **ASIME 2016 and 2018 white papers (Graps et al.)**: Asteroid-mining companies' questions answered by scientists; lists science knowledge gaps on survey, surface, subsurface, composition. Overlap: The community's own statement of need; must be cited to motivate the catalog. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00709)
- **NHATS, NEOfixer, ESA NEOCC Priority List**: Accessibility and observing windows (NHATS); astrometric follow-up prioritization for planetary defence (NEOfixer; NEOCC list being retired). Overlap: Serve access and orbit quality; none ranks by composition uncertainty. (https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/nhats.html)
- **Depue, Wolk & Farritor (macrogrants / claudestronomy), Nov 2026; Ilvarra agent, Oct 2026**: Claude/Codex agents searching public archive images; claimed two dwarf-planet candidates (no designation or write-up found) and 24 unmatched DECam objects sent to the MPC (self-reported). Overlap: Same agent-on-public-archives mode but discovery, not characterization; no published validation; sets the reputational context. (https://huggingnews.com/ai/will-depue-finds-second-potential-dwarf-planet-in-public-space-archive-99c7ee2e)
- **Company practice: AstroForge, Karman+, Planetary Resources, Deep Space Industries**: Pass/fail filters on delta-v, size, spin, and (where available) spectra or albedo; AstroForge buys telescope time; Karman+ lists 2022 DC5 and 2021 UH2. Overlap: Confirms the need (few candidates have spectra) and shows industry has in-house expertise; nothing probabilistic is public. (https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EPSC2024/EPSC2024-587.html)

### Opportunities

- **Scored calibration audit: predict class for held-out MANOS, UKIRT, Sanchez 2024 and MITHNEOS objects from orbit and H alone, then add one sparse datum at a time; report log loss, Brier score and reliability curves against the size-dependent base rate and against Jedicke-style, Granvik/NEOMOD and METEOMOD priors.** New: No proper-scoring evaluation of indirect class priors for NEOs was found; Deshapriya 2023 gives rules without probabilistic scoring and López-Oquendo 2026 only notes that source probabilities are weak. Feasibility: High. All labels and models are public (CDS tables, METEOMOD and NEOMOD code, SsODNet). A few hundred to about 1,500 labelled NEOs. Fits the pre-registration and null-result rules. Risk: The answer may be that orbit adds almost no information beyond the base rate. That is publishable but makes the catalog thin; label noise between surveys may dominate.
- **Frozen forward forecast: publish time-stamped per-object class probabilities for the NHATS list now and score them as Rubin colors, Euclid NIR classes, SPHEREx spectra and NEO Surveyor albedos arrive.** New: No pre-registered forecast of NEO composition was found; incoming surveys (about 4,000 NEOs with D >= 10 m from LSST, about 10^4 from Euclid) make the test real within one to three years. Feasibility: High once the audit model exists; needs a hash-stamped public release and a scoring protocol written in advance. Risk: Few NHATS objects will be re-observed by these surveys (tiny, long synodic periods), so the scored sample could be small; Dorsey & Granvik may publish class-specific models first.
- **Joined public catalog with an information-value ranking: for each accessible object, current posterior, what is actually known in the literature, next observing window (NHATS obs_start/obs_mag), and which observation would most reduce class uncertainty.** New: NHATS has no composition; NEOfixer ranks for astrometry only; the NEOCC list is being retired; Asterank and ECOCEL are deterministic and limited to typed objects. Feasibility: Medium-high. Mostly joins plus a simple expected-information calculation; site can be static. Risk: Perceived as Asterank again unless uncertainty is the headline and no monetary values appear; scientific weight rests on the audit.
- **Literature cross-match of all 7,226 NHATS objects (SsODNet/rocks, MANOS, UKIRT, Sergeyev 2023, NEOWISE, Spitzer) to replace the stale SBDB fields and state how many are truly uncharacterized.** New: Incremental, but the number is not published anywhere I found and the SBDB-based claim is wrong in spirit (SBDB types 327 NEOs; the literature has thousands). Feasibility: High; days of work. Risk: Low novelty; designation matching and conflicting classes need care.
- **Provenance classes for the lowest-delta-v objects: add lunar-ejecta, minimoon and artificial-object hypotheses to the prior for Earth-like orbits, using orbit, radiation-pressure A1 where measured, and published spectra (2024 PT5, Kamo'oalewa) as anchors.** New: Granvik, NEOMOD and METEOMOD have no lunar source; Alessi & Jedicke 2026 give a first population estimate but no per-object probabilities. Feasibility: Medium. Population numbers have orders-of-magnitude uncertainty; only a handful of anchor objects. Risk: Small numbers and weak constraints; partly covered by Kareta 2025 and Alessi & Jedicke 2026.
- **Use non-gravitational accelerations strictly as a filter and bound (natural versus artificial; plausible density range) with injection-recovery on astrometry, rather than as a composition or volatile indicator.** New: Existing work publishes A1/A2 and sample densities but not a catalog-wide, resource-framed screen with stated detection efficiency for NHATS-size objects. Feasibility: Low to medium. Needs orbit-fitting with full MPC astrometry and debiasing; signals exist for few objects (radiation pressure only at H > 27 with Earth encounters). Risk: Degenerate inference; specialists at JPL, NEODyS and ESA do the astrometry better; the dark-comet volatile reading was challenged in Nov 2026.

### Reasons it fails

- The prior collapses to the base rate: NHATS orbits are Earth-like, about 83% of small NEOs come via nu6 (Sanchez 2024), albedo is 'well mixed in orbital space' (Morbidelli 2020), and METEOMOD reportedly exceeds 50% for only about 20% of small NEOs, so 7,226 objects would get nearly identical probability vectors.
- The base rate itself is disputed and size-dependent: MANOS finds about one third S below 50 m, UKIRT finds a C/X-dominated debiased population, Sanchez 2024 finds 66% S. 'Validated against known answers' depends on which survey is treated as truth.
- Labels are noisy: four-band colors do not give unique classes, lightcurve correction moves the C fraction from 10% to 27%, and the same object (2011 CG2) was typed Cgh and Q.
- Class is not composition and composition is not resource: X-complex is degenerate without albedo; hydration appears on S and V types (McGraw 2026); Ch is only a proxy; surface OH is not bulk water; low-perihelion heating dehydrates.
- The validation set thins out where the targets are: NHATS median H is 26.2, MANOS mean H is about 25, and typed objects at H > 27 number in the tens.
- The 'none of the 286 has a taxonomy in SBDB' framing is an artifact of SBDB coverage (327 typed NEOs of 43,360) and a referee will reject it unless redone against the literature.
- The lowest-delta-v objects may not be main-belt asteroids: minimoons (2026 QQ8, 2020 CD3), lunar ejecta (2024 PT5, Kamo'oalewa) and possible spacecraft hardware sit at the top of the NHATS list, and no NEO source model includes them.
- Non-gravitational signals are not composition: Yarkovsky density is degenerate with thermal properties, obliquity and size; radiation pressure is seen only for H > 27 objects with Earth encounters; the volatile interpretation of dark comets was directly challenged on 6 Nov 2026; artificial-object confusion is documented.
- The metal channel does not reach the targets: Harris & Drube's 18 candidates are 0.36 to 3 km and mostly S/Q by spectra; 0 of the 286 lowest-delta-v objects have an SBDB albedo.
- The work can be scooped or superseded: Dorsey & Granvik are building class-specific NEO models, Karman+ and AstroForge have in-house expertise and telescope time, and Rubin, Euclid, SPHEREx and NEO Surveyor will label objects within one to three years.
- The Asterank trap: an interactive ranking with point values and no visible uncertainty will be dismissed by professionals on sight.
- Agent provenance is a liability right now: the nearest AI-agent neighbours are unverified discovery claims, the MPC warned about AI-assisted submissions in October 2026, and AAS Journals require AI-use disclosure.
