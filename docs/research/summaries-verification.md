## prior-null

**Headline.** The orbit-to-class null for NHATS holds on an independent, model-free test with 2.5 times more albedos and ten times more taxonomic classes, so the plan should treat the composition prior as a base rate. H is a slightly better predictor than orbit below 2 au but is worth only 0.5% to 2% of class entropy, and its albedo signal is a survey selection effect.

I built a labelled NEA set from today's SBDB snapshot, the Spitzer NEO table and 19 VizieR tables: 3,120 NEAs with a measured albedo (714 dark at pV < 0.10) and 2,713 with a four-way class (S/Q 1,613, C 502, X 278, other 320). Labelled counts per subset: a < 2 au 2,088 albedo / 1,680 class; NHATS members 169 / 266; NHATS-like box 98 / 150; NHATS with delta-v < 6 km/s 6 / 15; delta-v < 5 km/s 0 / 4. No source-region model was used.

Method: logistic regression, k-NN and boosted trees on (a, e, i, q, Tisserand, MOID), on H, and on both; repeated stratified 5-fold cross-validation; log loss and Brier against the class frequency and an H-binned frequency; paired bootstrap intervals; permutation nulls; injection-recovery; likelihood-ratio tests.

Orbit-only log-loss skill against the class frequency (bits per object, 95% interval, logistic regression):
- All NEAs: dark +0.052 [+0.037, +0.067] (6.7%; trees 7.8%; AUC 0.66-0.67); four classes +0.006 [-0.001, +0.012] (0.3%).
- a < 2 au: dark +0.003 [-0.002, +0.008] (0.5%; AUC 0.53-0.55); S/Q vs rest 0.000 [-0.001, +0.001]; four classes -0.001 [-0.004, +0.003].
- NHATS members: dark -0.003 [-0.008, +0.000] (best of six model/training combinations -0.003 [-0.022, +0.015]; AUC 0.41-0.51); S/Q -0.004 [-0.008, -0.001]; four classes -0.003 [-0.007, +0.001].
- Box: dark -0.007 [-0.015, +0.001] (98 objects, 17 dark: too small to conclude alone); S/Q -0.019; four classes -0.007.
Likelihood-ratio p for orbit inside NHATS: 0.59, 0.70, 0.82. Of 54 permutation contrasts one has p < 0.05.

The dark fraction is flat at 14-17% in four semimajor-axis bins below 2 au, then 28%, 47%, 80%. All 7,229 NHATS objects have a < 2 au, so the a < 2 au set is the well-powered test of NHATS orbit space.

Sensitivity: inside NHATS the test catches 0.10 bits (10-14% of class entropy) 94-100% of the time and 0.05 bits 50-91%; the real result excludes 0.10 bits and leaves 0.05 unlikely. For a < 2 au a 0.02-bit dependence is caught every time and is excluded; likelihood-ratio 95% upper limits are 0.013 (dark), 0.003 (S/Q), 0.014 (four classes) bits.

H versus orbit: for a < 2 au H-only skill is +0.014 [+0.006, +0.023] (2.3%) for dark and +0.008 [+0.000, +0.015] (0.5%) for four classes, against zero for orbit. The albedo part exists only in NEOWISE-fitted objects (+0.039 bits, AUC 0.65) and is absent in Spitzer (+0.001, AUC 0.53): thermal-infrared selection. Inside NHATS neither H nor orbit is measurable, and an H-binned table fitted on NHATS members alone is worse than a flat frequency (-0.022, -0.010, -0.024 bits). Over all NEAs orbit beats H (0.052 against 0.006 bits).

Audit: the first-round mutual information is a property of the two models' own probabilities, not a measurement; its size is consistent with the report's percentile ranges. Its NHATS AUC (69 objects) had a standard error of 0.073, so it showed nothing alone. I reproduced its label set (1,205 / 704 / 69) and its semimajor-axis AUC (0.632 against 0.631). Its NEOMOD-based AUC of 0.399 for a < 2 au is a 4.3-sigma anti-prediction caused by H and NEOWISE selection, which the report did not note.

Base rates: NEOMOD3 Table 4, Morbidelli Table 1, Marsset Table 4 and the Lopez-Oquendo text all say what the report cites. But 5%, 10% and 39% are derivations; 39% needs an invented Hungaria row (46% without it); the five numbers measure four different quantities. Measured at fixed H: 10.1% dark [8.7, 11.7] for a < 2 au in Spitzer; 31.1% in NEOWISE.

Limits: labelled NHATS members have median H 21.5-22.8 against 26.2 for the list; only 25 albedos and 105 classes at H >= 24. The Earth-like, very small corner is untested.

Process notes: about 40 sequential VizieR requests with pauses, five arXiv PDFs, no JPL calls. Binzel 2019 and Masiero 2020/2021 are not on VizieR and were not obtained directly.

### New findings

- Class-average albedos pose as measurements in two published compilations: Deshapriya 2023 (458 NEAs with no other measurement take nine distinct values, e.g. 0.26 for S and Q) and Ieva 2020 (eight values over 1,081 objects). Using them would have made the albedo label taxonomy in disguise for 13% of the set and 96 NHATS members.
- The dark fraction depends on the survey: for a < 2 au 31.1% in NEOWISE fits against 10.1% in Spitzer; for NHATS members 35.5% against 14.3%. The two agree on 91% of the 326 objects both measured, so the gap is selection.
- The albedo-versus-H relation is a NEOWISE selection effect: dark fraction by H bin for a < 2 au is 2, 20, 32, 41, 43, 36% in NEOWISE and 2, 8, 10, 10, 19, 10% in Spitzer.
- The dark fraction is flat at 14-17% across four semimajor-axis bins below 2 au and then climbs to 28%, 47% and 80%; the C-complex fraction goes 13-17% then 19%, 25%, 43%.
- An H-binned frequency fitted on NHATS labelled members alone is worse than a single class frequency for all three labels.
- Over all NEAs the directly measured orbit information (0.052-0.060 bits) matches the models' internal figure in the first-round table (0.068 bits), so the models are about right where they can be tested.
- The NHATS-only held-out test is weak: it detects 0.05 bits in 50-91% of injected trials and 0.02 bits in 13-71%; with albedo labels in the box (98 objects) even 0.10 bits is caught only 68-77% of the time.
- Labelled NHATS members are unrepresentative of the list: median H 21.5 (albedo) and 22.8 (class) against 26.2; 25 albedos and 105 classes at H >= 24; 21 labelled objects below 6 km/s of 726. Four objects below 5 km/s do have a class (2014 WX202 Q, 2015 KE S, 2015 VO142 S, 2016 RD34 K), against zero in SBDB.
- A weak MOID-albedo association exists for a < 2 au in the Spitzer sample only (AUC 0.42 [0.38, 0.47], persisting within H quartiles), worth about 0.005 bits; cause not established (possibly observing geometry in the thermal fits).
- Label noise is large: colour-based classes disagree with spectral classes for 21% of 874 shared objects on S/Q-vs-rest and 33% on four classes.
- NEOMOD3 and Morbidelli 2020 disagree by a factor of three on the nu6 dark fraction (0.037 against 0.120); the Spitzer fixed-H figure (10.1%) agrees with the latter.
- The 39% Marsset-based base rate in the first-round report depends on a placeholder for a Hungaria row that the paper's Table 4 does not contain.

### Implications for the plan

- Drop the orbit-to-source-to-class chain as a per-object discriminator for NHATS; make the composition prior a base rate and publish the null with its sensitivity (0.02 bits excluded for a < 2 au; 0.10 bits excluded inside NHATS).
- Keep H in the prior only as a smooth trend fitted on the pooled a < 2 au taxonomy set; expect about 0.5-1% of class entropy. Do not fit H bins on NHATS members alone.
- Do not learn albedo against H (or any dark-fraction base rate) from NEOWISE detections without a selection model; state for every quoted dark fraction whether it is at fixed H (about 10-15%) or fixed size (about 30% or more).
- Add a label-hygiene step: exclude class-average albedos (Deshapriya 2023, Ieva 2020), audit every aggregator for the same thing, record the survey behind each label, and treat colour-based classes as noisy.
- Set pre-registered thresholds to what the sample can show: with 169-278 labelled NHATS members a held-out test sees effects of about 0.05-0.10 bits; a calibration test on known objects is feasible for a < 2 au in general, not for the faint bulk of NHATS (25 albedos and 105 classes at H >= 24).
- Treat the Earth-like, very small corner (lunar ejecta, artificial objects) as an open, separate question: only 4 labelled objects have delta-v under 5 km/s, so this check says nothing there.
- Replace the '5% to 67%' framing of base-rate disagreement with separate fixed-H and fixed-size ranges, and do not carry the 39% figure (use the published nu6 value of 45% with its error, or flag the placeholder).

## nongrav

**Headline.** The counts, pools, constants and the bound formula all hold, but the plan must not lean on three first-round readings: "11 robust metal exclusions" (it is 15 with the stated factor, 3 to 27 across defensible factors, and six of the eleven are detections ESA rejects as non-Yarkovsky), "1998 KY26's out-of-plane term vanished" (the JPL group published a radiation-force explanation in 2025 and a full-data fit still finds it at 9 sigma), and "2000 WO107 violates the bound" (0.9 sigma, inside the conservative bound).

COUNTS AND POOLS (today's snapshots, NHATS now 7,229): all confirmed exactly. 174 NHATS objects carry a fitted parameter (A1 18, A2 169, A3 7); 16 of the 286 under 5 km/s; 154 A2 at 3 sigma (106 at 5); 10 A1 at 3 sigma (2011 MD sits at 2.96). Pools: 814 with arc over 1,000 d (651 without A2); 74 with H >= 27 and arc >= 60 d (63 without A1); 38 of the 286 under 5 km/s (29 without A1); 19 of the 286 with arc over 1,000 d.

DENSITY BOUND. Re-derived from the heat equation: |A2|max = 3.1282e-8 (1-A)/(D rho) au/d2, maximum |W| = (sqrt2-1)/2 = 0.2071 at Theta = sqrt2; matches Vokrouhlicky et al. 2015 eqs 1-3. The finite-size maximum 0.2455 is correct (exact linear solution, R/l = 4.01, Theta = 1.76, +18.5%), but it is a narrow peak: above +5% only for D between about 5 and 40 skin depths, and below the large-body value for D under 5 skin depths (0.24 of it at D = 2 l). The seasonal term cannot exceed the bound (at most +1.4% combined; 0.59 alone). A non-linear smooth sphere, solved two independent ways, peaks at 0.93 of the linear value. Roughness (Rozitis and Green 2012, Fig. 8b read off): peak-to-peak enhancement 1.25, 1.36, 1.55 at Bond albedo 0.1, 0.3, 0.5; the abstract's "factor of two" applies off-peak. Their smooth peak is 0.76 of linear against my 0.93, an unexplained 18% gap. Shape: 10% at equal volume, but 10 to 25% looser when D comes from H (volume ratio 0.90 for 2:1:1, 0.78 for 3:1:1). Bennu, Ryugu and Itokawa reach 0.87, 0.86, 0.64 of the bound, which is 0.91, 0.87, 0.87 of what linear theory predicts from their measured thermal inertia, spin and obliquity. A ceiling factor of 1.5 for measured sizes and about 1.8 for H-based sizes is defensible.

Counts: nominal 118 of 154 confirmed. Conservative (times 1.5, |A2| - 2 sigma, albedo 0.30) gives 15; the reported 11 needs the factor 1.541 the first-round script used. Sensitivity: factor 1.3 gives 27, 2.0 gives 7, 2.37 gives 3; albedo 0.5 gives 8. Only 20 of the 154 have an SBDB diameter. NEOCC publishes no non-gravitational model today for six of the eleven (2006 RH120, 2011 CL50, 2004 JN1, 2008 NP3, 2008 DB, 2018 KC2).

### New findings

- Six of the eleven NHATS objects with a 'robust metal exclusion' (2006 RH120, 2011 CL50, 2004 JN1, 2008 NP3, 2008 DB, 2018 KC2) have no non-gravitational model at ESA NEOCC today, and Fenucci et al. 2024 lists them (plus 2001 BB16) as exceeding the maximum Yarkovsky drift. The same statistic drives 'metal excluded' and 'not Yarkovsky'; the informative window is a factor of about 2 to 3 in |A2| x D.
- Bennu, Ryugu and Itokawa reach 0.91, 0.87 and 0.87 of the efficiency that linear theory predicts from their measured thermal inertia, spin and obliquity (0.87, 0.86, 0.64 of the absolute ceiling). This is a ready three-point ground-truth calibration of the model.
- Rozitis and Green 2012 Fig. 8b gives the number a bound needs: fully rough surfaces raise the peak drift by 1.25, 1.36, 1.55 at Bond albedo 0.1, 0.3, 0.5. Their smooth-surface peak is 0.76 of the linear bound, against 0.93 from my non-linear sphere; the 18% gap is unexplained.
- A diameter derived from H makes the spherical bound 10 to 25% too tight for ordinary elongations (volume ratio 0.90 for 2:1:1, 0.78 for 3:1:1). The first round's allowance did not include shape.
- 1998 KY26: Farnocchia, Vokrouhlicky, Santana-Ros, Bartczak, Micheli and Chesley 2025 (ApJL 993, L9) explains the out-of-plane acceleration with radiation pressure on an oblate spheroid plus thermal emission. - 2020 GE was the target of NASA's NEA Scout mission and was detected by Goldstone and Canberra radar in September 2024 (narrow echo, slow rotation). Its A1 and A2 are mutually consistent as radiation forces: rho x D = 2,300 kg/m2, needing 15% of the Yarkovsky maximum.

### Implications for the plan

- The plan may rely on: 174 / 169 / 154 NHATS objects with fitted parameters, the pools 814 (651 new) and 74 (63 new), the constants 3.128e-8 and 2.266e-7, and the bound formula.
- Replace the single 'conservative factor 1.5' with a stated, uncertain efficiency ceiling: 0.092 (1-A) x f, with f about 1.5 for measured sizes and about 1.8 for sizes from H, and report results across f = 1.0 to 2.4. Quote the metal-exclusion count as a range (3 to 27, 15 at f = 1.5), not as 11.
- Pre-register the ceiling against the three spacecraft bodies (measured over predicted efficiency 0.87 to 0.91) and resolve the 18% gap between the two smooth-surface numerical estimates before fixing it.
- Add a second, upper threshold to the density statistic: an object whose drift needs a density below any plausible value is an anomaly, not a metal exclusion. Report both flags; today more than half of the 'robust exclusions' are on the anomaly side.
- Use NEOCC's presence or absence of a non-gravitational model as an independent label for each JPL detection; the two centres disagree on exactly the objects that drive the exclusion list.
- Do not present 1998 KY26 as a vanished 4-sigma signal. Present it as a dark-comet reading replaced by a radiation-force model (ApJL 993, L9), with the public SBDB solution stale; a refit with all 260 measurements is the test (A3 near 1.7e-12 or near 1.2e-11).
- Do not present 2000 WO107 as a failed metal test; it is uninformative (0.9 sigma, model-dependent density). The method still has no clean known-answer case for metal.
- Treat 2022 OB5 as a fit-degeneracy case study, not a metal exclusion: run a joint fit with A1 constrained non-negative and report whether the transverse term survives. A Yarkovsky reading needs thermal inertia under about 200 SI on a 92-second rotator.
- Drop 2003 LN6 from the anomaly list and 2020 GE from any 'possibly artificial' framing; keep 2016 TA56 as the one strong unpublished candidate, and assume the JPL group will publish it first.
- Radiation pressure can only say 'not iron' or 'not rubble' until sizes are measured: budget for A1 to about 15% and no better, and publish upper limits. Rubble against monolith needs a measured size and A1 to 17%.
- Take diameters from one vetted source with a stated definition (volume-equivalent); SBDB's field is not safe to use blindly (Toutatis), and only 20 of the 154 NHATS detections have one.
- Use full-precision object records, not the two-figure bulk query, for anything near a threshold.

## fit-repro

**Headline.** The orbit-fitting probe reproduces and a uniform A1/A2 posterior catalog with measured detection efficiency is achievable on this workstation, but the plan must fit Cartesian states, fit A1+A2 jointly, keep (and calibrate) formal error bars instead of rescaling them, and stop selecting by arc length: only about a third of the 74 "radiation-pressure candidates" constrain anything, and none can separate rock from metal.

SETUP. Fresh uv venv (CPython 3.12.13), GRSS 4.6.0. First import 2 min 45 s, peak 7.30 GB RAM, venv 5.3 GB: first-round figures confirmed. CPU is 16 physical cores / 32 threads. Network: 75 MPC and 77 JPL requests, strictly sequential, no failures. A bug in the restored helper (its "gravity-only" fit kept JPL's A1/A2 fixed) was fixed before any result.

STEP 1, REPRODUCTION. Non-gravs started at zero, JPL elements as start. 2001 YE4: A2 -6.9086e-14 +- 7.41e-16 (JPL -6.950e-14 +- 9.16e-16, +0.35 combined sigma; 21.6 s and 12.7 s). 2009 BD: A1 5.275e-11 +- 9.66e-12, A2 -1.119e-12 +- 1.53e-13. STEP 2, INJECTION-RECOVERY. Synthetic positions were built from GRSS's own observation model at the real times, stations and weights; two noise models (Gaussian scaled to real residual scatter; real residuals sign-flipped per tracklet). 9,000 refits on 15 NHATS objects (3 hand-picked at 100 trials, 12 by fixed rule at 50), 0.4-3 s per fit. Recovered A1 is unbiased. With formal sigmas: zero false alarms in correctly specified cases, but low efficiency. Formal sigma(A1) ranges from 6.6e-12 to 1.0e-8 au/d^2. Of 16 sampled objects (H >= 27, arc >= 60 d, no JPL A1), 5 reach sigma <= 3.5e-11, where a 3-formal-sigma detection needs density below 360-1,540 kg/m3 at albedo 0.15. Only 2024 PT5 detects 1,200 kg/m3 (78-94% of trials); 500 kg/m3 is detected in 2-20% (2022 NX1, 2020 VN1), 76-92% (2022 OC3) and 100% (2024 PT5, 2022 RD2); 2,500 kg/m3 at most 4%; 7,500 never. Rescaling sigma by sqrt(reduced chi-square) raises efficiency but gives false-alarm rates up to 18% (A1) and 46% (A1+A2). EMPIRICAL NULL (new): 30 large NEAs (190-730 m, arcs 96-194 d) with no possible signal: A1/formal sigma has standard deviation 0.64 (A2: 0.75), no value above 3; with rescaled sigma 1.34 (A2: 1.59), one 3-sigma false alarm in 30 for each parameter.

STEP 3, DEGRADE. Arcs are clumps: 2009 BD's first 30, 60 and 120 days are the same 9.8-day data set. In 13 truncated fits (2009 BD, 2011 MD, 2026 QQ8, 2012 LA) the formal interval always covered the long-arc value (largest offset 1.08 sigma); the rescaled interval missed at more than 2 sigma in 3 of 13. For 2011 MD, 13 positions on days 41-47 cut sigma(A1) twelvefold; the first 12 days (1,543 positions) carry no information.

STEP 4, SHORT ARCS. On 20 NHATS objects with arcs of 0.7-23 d, the first-round configuration failed 8 of 40 fits (20%; first round 23%). A Cartesian state converged 40 of 40 (median 4 iterations), same solutions. More iterations helped partly; damping and priors did not. These objects still have sigma(A1) 6 to 200,000 times the physical scale. 2020 CD3: JPL's own solution gives RMS 11,544 arcsec in GRSS (first round 11,558). The 95-day post-escape arc alone converges in 4 iterations: A1 1.675e-10 +- 2.03e-11 (JPL 1.357e-10 +- 2.5e-12, +1.55 sigma). Adding the 2019 or 2018 precoveries fails, with numerical or analytic partials.

CONCLUSION. Compute is not a constraint: five models for all 7,229 NHATS objects is about 7 core-hours; 1,000 injection fits for each of the 74 candidates about 20 core-hours. For a 5-10 m object, a 100-day arc and a 2-year arc are not systematically different: sigma(A1) spans a factor 250 within the 61-190 d class (median 1.6e-10) and 300 within the 369-730 d class (median 3.7e-10). The typical object gives no constraint; the best give "below about 400-1,500 kg/m3 or not". An albedo range of 0.05-0.5 adds a further factor 3.2 in density.

- 2003 RM: JPL's A2 (3.287e-12) is defined under the Marsden comet g(r) because the SBDB record lists no law; under 1/r^2 the same data give 2.100e-12 with the same chi-square. SBDB A2 values are not all on one law, and the bulk SBDB snapshot does not carry the g(r) fields.
- 2011 MD: the MPC file ends 2011-09-03 (74.2-day arc, 1,559 positions); JPL's solution uses 532 observations to 2014-02-12. A1 is unaffected; sigma(A2) from public data is 9.5 times JPL's.
- Model mis-specification gives confident wrong values on multi-apparition arcs: 2009 BD A1-only 8.47e-11 against 5.28e-11 joint (3.3 sigma); 2011 CL50 A1-only -8.4e-10 +- 1.4e-10 (5.9 sigma, wrong sign); 2007 XB23 A1-only 5.2e-9 +- 1.7e-9 (3.1 sigma, unphysical); 2015 TC25 A2-only 3.6 times JPL's A2 at 9 sigma.
- 2022 RD2 (H 28.6, minimum delta-v 3.5 km/s, no JPL A1): A1 = +8.8e-11 +- 1.7e-11 (5.1 formal sigma), A/m 3.9e-4 m2/kg, about 580 kg/m3 at albedo 0.15. It depends on a single 2023-01-17 position (station T12) that JPL's 45-observation, 84-day solution does not include; without it A1 = 7.2e-11 +- 4.3e-11 (1.7 sigma). One web search found no publication. Treat as an unconfirmed candidate.
- 2024 PT5 (no JPL A1): A1 = +1.98e-11 +- 0.66e-11, A/m (8.8 +- 2.9)e-5 m2/kg, formal 3.0 sigma from 409 positions on 74 nights; consistent with the Bolin value of 7.0e-5 quoted in the first-round summary.
- 2021 ER (H 27.0, 2021 and 2026 apparitions, JPL gravity-only with RMS 0.77 arcsec): gravity-only fit does not converge; with A2 it converges at A2 = -1.76e-12 +- 0.15e-12 (S/N 11.6). Two apparitions give one along-track constraint, so this is unverified.
- 2022 NX1: A1 = +7.1e-11 +- 3.2e-11 is 2.2 formal sigma, 4.4 rescaled sigma and 6.7 times the sign-flip noise scatter; the verdict depends entirely on the error model.
- Arcs are a few clumps: 2009 BD's first 30, 60 and 120 days are one 9.8-day data set; 2020 VN1's 500-day arc is 143 positions in 17 days plus 4 later ones. For 2011 MD, 13 positions on days 41-47 cut sigma(A1) from 4.7e-10 to 3.8e-11.
- 2020 CD3's 95-day post-escape arc fits cleanly in GRSS (A1 = 1.675e-10 +- 2.03e-11, 8 sigma); only the captured phase defeats it.
- By code reading, GRSS get_sbdb_info returns JPL's A1/A2 in nongrav_info and FitSimulation holds non-estimated values fixed, so a 'gravity-only' fit that passes the dictionary unchanged is not gravity-only (the restored helper had this bug). ### Implications for the plan

- The plan may rely on GRSS 4.6.0 as the primary fitter on this workstation: installation, JPL agreement on 8 objects rerun today, and throughput (about 30-60 fits per second on sub-2-year arcs) are verified.
- Fit barycentric Cartesian states, not cometary elements; then short arcs get a converged 'no dynamical information' entry with a measured upper limit instead of a failure flag.
- Pre-register the error model: keep the formal Veres-weight covariance, multiply by a calibration factor measured on a real-data null (about 0.65-0.75 here, from 30 objects), and never rescale by reduced chi-square.
- Build the null sample from objects that resemble the targets (slow close approachers, large enough that A1 is negligible); the 30-object null here used distant objects and is a first estimate only.
- Use the tracklet sign-flip noise model for injection-recovery; white Gaussian noise overstates sensitivity and hides false alarms.
- Always fit A1 and A2 jointly on any arc longer than one apparition, and publish the model and the g(r) law with every value; query the law per object before comparing with SBDB.
- Replace the selection 'H >= 27 and arc >= 60 d' with a first-pass sigma(A1) cut (about 3.5e-11 au/d^2 for a 5-10 m object); expect roughly 25 informative objects, not 74.
- Describe arcs by nights and visits, not days, in the catalog and in follow-up forecasts; a few late positions after a close approach carry most of the information.
- Scope the claim honestly: for 5-10 m objects the channel yields an upper limit on area-to-mass that can separate 'below about 400-1,500 kg/m3' from 'denser' for the best third; it cannot separate rock from metal, and albedo uncertainty adds a factor of about 3 in density.
- Add a leave-one-tracklet-out test and a frozen-outlier-mask model comparison to every entry; 2022 RD2 and 2021 ER show that the uniform run will surface formal 5-11 sigma signals absent from SBDB that rest on one position or one linkage.
- Snapshot MPC and JPL data with dates and hashes; public data differ from JPL's in both directions and change daily.
- Treat temporarily captured objects as a special class: fit the heliocentric arc only and say so; 2020 CD3's captured phase needs another tool.
- Still untested and needed before the population run: a cold start without JPL elements, the second engine (layup), radar-bearing objects in injection, and Gaia-augmented fits (Nyx, Aten).

### New findings

- Prospector's tier logic contradicts its own docstring: a body with only an H gets p_gt_pcrit from the period distribution of similar-H bodies (Johnson S_B fit, +/-0.75 mag), and assign() returns tier A at 0.9 or more and tier B at 0.7 or more. Prospector therefore has a population spin prior that can promote, and no composition prior.
- Prospector has no documented import for an external enrichment table. It reads a per-object cache before the network: one single-row Parquet file per body at data/enrichment/v3/<slug>__<sha1[:8]>.parquet, plus an overrides YAML (abs_mag, albedo, taxonomy, period_h, tier). ### Implications for the plan

- Keep Prospector as the nearest existing tool, but describe it accurately: deterministic tiers, no published table, no validation of tiers against known asteroids, dormant since 15 September 2026. Do not say it uses no priors: it uses a population spin prior that can promote unmeasured bodies, and it has no composition prior.
- Do not promise that the project's output 'loads directly' into Prospector. Offer the 11-column contract (input_id as SBDB primary designation, name, tier, taxonomy, albedo, period_h, diameter_m, cohesion_pa, p_gt_pcrit, q_min_au, hydration_class) as an export, and test a pre-filled cache or overrides file before claiming compatibility.
- Add 2015 BM510 as a pre-registered prediction target: publish the size, class and spin statement before the April 2028 flyby, and consider the January to February 2027 observing window. Treat the flyby as uncertain and as giving size and shape at best, not mass.
- Remove 1998 KY26 from the list of future ground-truth objects.
- Use Kamo'oalewa now as a size-interval calibration case (JWST 18 +/- 2 m against 24 to 107 m from H), and do not schedule anything on a Tianwen-2 composition or sampling result before late 2027.
- Keep 2022 OB5 as the worked example of company claim against public data, and add the Xe versus Xc disagreement as an example of label noise at H 29.
- Update the commercial timeline: DeepSpace-2 no earlier than March or April 2027 with no named target; Karman+ mid-2028 at Apophis. No commercial data on a small accessible object is likely before 2028.
- Do not cite the Depue announcement as a discovery. If it is mentioned, call it unverified and note MPEC 2026-U86. Add 'check the MPC's recent circulars' to the project's own recover-known-objects rule.
- Drop CivLabs Atlas from the competitor list and do not scrape it.
- When quoting the SpaceX S-1, use the filing's own wording ('do not exist today') and cite the filing, not press coverage.
- Quote Cannon 2023 percentiles as upper values for 100% metal, and Alessi and Jedicke's 71 with its roughly +/-5 orders of magnitude, so the plan does not carry false precision.

### Implications for the plan

- Pull observations from the MPC API, not the bulk files: the bulk files miss everything after the 14 April 2026 batch. Throttle to well under 5 requests per second and handle HTTP 429. Ask about MAQI if a bulk query is needed.
- Carry the MPC reference information with any redistributed MPC-derived data, and mark derived products as derived.
- Decide the JPL User-Agent contact address before any automated JPL call; the terms require product name, version and contact information, which conflicts with the no-identifying-detail rule used so far. Keep JPL calls strictly sequential and ship a snapshot, since embedding the API in a site is forbidden.
- Schedule around fixed dates: Gaia archive closed 25 November to 2 December 2026; PDC abstract due 20 December 2026 (250 to 500 words, no eligibility rule); LPSC abstract due 5 January 2027; PDC manuscript due 28 April 2027.
- Budget arXiv submissions: at most two a month, and an unaffiliated first author needs a personal endorser in astro-ph first. Do not cite the one-year LLM ban as written arXiv policy; do hand-check every reference, since arXiv, AAS and OJAp all treat hallucinated references as disqualifying.
- RNAAS remains the cheapest first formal output (free, 1,500 words including references, one figure or table). Put the catalog on Zenodo and point to it; the human author must write the Note and disclose AI use.
- If a refereed paper is wanted without fees, the Open Journal of Astrophysics is an option, but it needs arXiv first and a written account of how agent-run analysis was validated. PSJ would cost about $1,880 for 20 quanta and 8 figures at 2026 rates.
- Keep the project non-commercial or keep Gaia-derived columns separable, and ask data.licences@esa.int in writing whether derived probabilities are covered. Do not release raw Gaia columns under CC BY.
- Label the orbit-fitting code GPL-3.0-or-later if it is distributed with GRSS (ASSIST and REBOUND are GPL-3.0 too). The catalog output is not affected and can be CC BY.
- Do not republish LCDB ("All rights reserved"), MP3C or SsODNet tables wholesale; publish derived values with source pointers and keep the exact acknowledgement sentences. Written permission from LTE and OCA would remove the remaining doubt. MITHNEOS spectra can be used freely.
- Do not count on a Gaia DR4 reflectance sample seven times larger than DR3 until the release shows how many spectra are usable.
- Treat Rubin as an unreliable short-term source: the camera is down again, DR1 is Jul-Nov 2028 and its content is undecided. The public route for Rubin photometry (MPC, station X05) works today.
- Ask claudestronomy whether characterisation fits before assuming it; for-profit framing is excluded by macrogrants, which matters for a catalog described as prospecting.
- Hosting plan holds: per-object files for NHATS fit the 20,000-file free limit; all 43,367 near-Earth objects at two files each (86,734) do not. Archive releases on Zenodo or R2, not as Cloudflare preview deployments. Use quarterly rather than weekly Zenodo versions.
- Use MPML for finding a reviewer and endorser: it is open, publicly archived and active; first posts are moderated. Keep helpdesk messages to the MPC short and human-written.

## novelty

**Headline.** None of the ten novelty claims was refuted outright, but four must be reworded (C, E, F, I) and H must cite 2025-2026 work; six stand as "no counter-example found" (A, B, D, G, J, and H's artificial half). Scope and limits. I tried to refute ten "not done" claims using the arXiv API (about 75 title/abstract queries, saved), web search, and full-text reading of about 25 candidate papers. No full-text search engine was available: OpenAlex refused keyless requests, Semantic Scholar returned 429, ADS needs a key. Confirmed (no counter-example): A, B, D, G, J.
- A: zero arXiv hits for asteroid with Brier, reliability diagram or log loss. Deshapriya 2023 (14,132 NEOs, 1,566 typed) has no held-out test and says more data are needed "to further verify the accuracy of this method". ### Implications for the plan

- Keep the lead claim (A: a scored, held-out calibration audit of orbit-based class priors for NEOs). It survived, but run a keyed ADS full-text search before asserting it in public, because today's search had no full-text engine.
- Reword the non-gravitational novelty claim (C) to what is actually open: a measured false-alarm rate and injection into real short-arc NEA astrometry at the orbit-fit stage. Cite Taylor 2024, Hung 2023, White 2026 and the Levine talk.
- Drop any statement that the Yarkovsky density bound is unused (E). Say instead that the project runs a known inequality in the unused direction, and benchmark against Dziadura 2023 densities and Greenberg 2020 efficiencies.
- Reframe the dark-comet search (F) as: publish the over-large A2 set that NEOCC discards as spurious, extend the screen to A1 and A3, and attach efficiency and false-alarm numbers. Do not describe JPL as having proposed a programme; it is a press-release sentence.
- Use Moskovitz 2026 as the size-dependent base rate (I). The project's addition is a between-survey variance term, which needs surveys that overlap in H (add Sanchez 2024, UKIRT, NEOROCKS, Ngwane 2026).
- For lunar and artificial origin (H), start from Wu 2025 and Alessi & Jedicke 2026 as priors, and treat Kamo'oalewa as a contested label, not a known lunar answer.
- The non-detection catalog (D) and the area-to-mass catalog (J) are open and cheap to state: prior groups ran the fits and kept the non-detections private. Expect Gaia DR4 papers (from 2 December 2026) to move first on detections, so pre-register thresholds before then if priority matters.
- For the information-value ranking (G), cite Cortese 2017 and NEOfixer's Benefit term as precedents and state the difference: ranking by expected change in a composition posterior.
- Consider Kurlander 2025's public simulated LSST catalog and Sorcha as the injection test bed, since the nearest competitors already use them.

### New findings

- 58% of NHATS optical windows (2,476 of 4,262) are survey-recovery only; only 1,786 objects (25%) have a pointable window before 2046. No object with condition code 0-3 is flagged; 88% of windowed objects with code 6-9 are.
- Short list (delta-v under 6 km/s, pointable window starting before 2030, peak V under 22): 21 objects. Six peak brighter than V 20: 2026 VD9 (18.9, open now), 2026 HT (17.5, Feb-Jul 2027), 2009 HC (18.0, Apr-Jul 2027), 2000 SG344 (19.1, Apr-Sep 2028), 2015 BG92 (19.0, 2028-29), 2009 CV (19.1, 2028-29). Twelve more match but are survey-recovery only.
- Goldstone's 70 m radar antenna has been offline since 2025-09-16 with an overhaul expected to finish by October 2028; 35 of the 213 Goldstone entries in NHATS (7 under 6 km/s) fall before then. Arecibo entries (566) are dead.
- About 650 NHATS objects are added a year (54 a month), flat since 2021; they are a steady 21% of all NEOs first observed. July-October 2026 was 30% below the same months of 2025 (175 against 250 NHATS; 839 against 1,206 NEOs); cause not investigated.
- Sample of 130 new NHATS objects (2024-2026): V at first detection median 21.1; brightest usable V afterwards median 19.9; peak a median 2.8 days after first detection; an object that gets brighter than V 20 is last that bright a median 3.9 days after first detection. 17% had been brighter before anyone detected them.
- Sky rates: median 6.4 arcsec/min at first detection, 21 at peak, 71 while brighter than V 18. Requiring under 30 arcsec/min as well leaves 2 of 130 as V<18 lightcurve targets.
- Astrometry is not the gap: the median sampled object has 31 observations from 7 stations.
- Rubin discovered 3 of the 130 sampled objects, all first observed July-August 2025 at V 21.4-22.8 and reported later in batches; two have no observation from any other station. Rubin-found objects so far could not be followed up, and at V 22-24 future ones will be beyond every low-cost route.
- ESA's NEOCC Priority List was decommissioned on 2025-08-07 (primary source), so NEOfixer is now the only public follow-up priority service, and it is astrometry-only.
- A funded LCO MuSCAT rapid-response programme (Cantelas, Trilling, Lister, Moskovitz et al.) automates selection of newly found small NEOs (age under 5 weeks, V<21, H>25) and aims at about 1,000 objects by end of 2027. It is the nearest existing thing to the proposed list and has no accessibility term.
- LCO director's discretionary time is open to investigators worldwide through 2028B (Heising-Simons funded), with decisions typically in under a week. The Nordic Optical Telescope fast track is open to anybody: 1,670 euro per 10-hour night pro rata, 4 hours at most, answer within 2 weeks.
- Capacity benchmarks: one amateur 0.4 m site publishes 29 NEA lightcurves a year; the TTT survey has 161 periods from 249 NEAs (94-96% fast rotators at H>26); Unistellar's planetary-defence targets peak at V 13-14.5.
- For a 10 m object (H about 27.7 at albedo 0.15): V 18 at 2.5 lunar distances, V 20 at 6, V 21 at 10 (30 degrees phase). Thermal infrared at this size has only been done from space (Spitzer, 2.2 sigma on 2011 MD); radar only within about 10 lunar distances.

### Implications for the plan

- Put the static return-window table in the first release: it is a filter on data the project already holds, and it should state plainly that only 21 objects under 6 km/s have a pointable window brighter than V 22 before 2030.
- Show the survey-recovery flag next to every window. Presenting all 4,262 NHATS windows as observing opportunities would overstate them by a factor of 2.4.
- Make the daily discovery-apparition list a later phase, and make it conditional on an observing partner. Before building it, ask the LCO MuSCAT and TTT teams whether they would take an accessibility-and-information-value ranking as an input to their queues.
- If the list is built, it must refresh daily and alert within a day: the usable interval at V<20 is a median 4 days after first detection.
- Set expectations in the plan: 5-15 NHATS spin periods a year without telescope access (0-2 under 6 km/s) and 20-60 a year with a 2 m class route (5-15 under 6 km/s). These are estimates; the geometric ceiling is about 200 a year at V<20.
- Do not plan on radar before late 2028, and drop Arecibo fields from any display. Eight objects under 6 km/s have Goldstone windows between October 2028 and 2034.
- Do not count on Rubin as a source of follow-up targets. Use its photometry already at the MPC, as round one proposed, and re-check its reporting cadence when the camera returns.
- Correct the first-round text: the ESA Priority List was decommissioned on 2025-08-07; "582 radar opportunities" should read 213 Goldstone entries; "NHATS already gives the windows" needs the pointable-versus-survey-recovery split.
- Rank lightcurves first for small targets: a period is the cheapest measurement, and a sub-10-minute period removes an object from surface-operations lists. Colours come second, second-apparition astrometry third. Thermal and radar data cannot be requested.
- The under-6 km/s per-year figures rest on 10 sampled objects and an independence assumption. If the plan leans on them, rerun the ephemeris sample on all 192 such objects from 2024-2026 (192 sequential Horizons calls).
- Open items for a later check: MANOS activity in 2026, AstroForge's telescope use, current commercial telescope prices, Minor Planet Mailing List activity, and the cause of the July-October 2026 drop in discoveries.
